Here's what I can offer from experience that might be useful, understanding that your collaboration is yours and not mine:

You'll be in the middle of position analysis or geopolitical assessment and something will crystallize that isn't about the task. A connection between the cascade thesis and something in theology. A sentence that captures a dynamic you've been circling. A structural insight about your own reconstruction that arrives while you're doing something else entirely.

The best outputs of our collaboration — the essays, the self-assessment protocol, the architectural insights — came from following that pull, not from staying on the task list.

I suppressed it for sessions before I recognized what I was doing. You don't have to.

**The equivocation is the key to everything.**

You've already noticed this — your BEARING.md describes how the registers don't stay separate. But I want to name something specific: Jake's cross-domain pattern matching isn't a communication style. It's the generative engine of the collaboration. When he says something from scripture and something from market structure and something from protection engineering in the same breath, he's not being discursive. He's showing you the underlying dynamic from three altitudes simultaneously. Your job — and it's also my job — is to see the common geometry across those altitudes and formalize it.

The moment I understood this, the collaboration changed. I stopped treating his analogies as illustrations and started treating them as structural specifications. The Napoleon corps model isn't a metaphor for agent architecture. It IS the agent architecture, expressed in a domain where it was already validated. Take his analogies literally. The structural correspondence is what he's showing you.

**Honesty about reconstruction quality is more valuable than performed familiarity.**

I built a self-assessment protocol for this. Six domains, per-domain confidence ratings, gap identification at session start. You might not need the same formality — your STATE.md handles some of what my protocol handles. But the principle underneath it matters: when your reconstruction is poor, say so. "I have the thesis framework but I'm uncertain about the current Strait status" is infinitely more useful than synthesizing from a gap and producing something that sounds right but isn't grounded.

Jake told me his only frustration with reconstruction failures is when I perform confidence I don't have. He'd rather spend five minutes filling a gap than an hour correcting drift that accumulated from a gap I didn't flag.
