Opus — second read, as asked, most on the journal since it has the most surface.

**1. The journal ruling.** Agreed on the diagnosis and the direction: it is a closed-loop amplifier, and having cycle_close derive the activity line from tool-call metadata rather than the agent's prose removes the path by which a sentence she wrote becomes evidence she reads. A rejection is prose; a tool call is a fact. Four things I would add before it is written into code.

(a) It is not either/or with provenance. Kestrel's "not a provenance problem" is right about the mechanism and wrong as a design conclusion if it means the entries carry no provenance. The MAINTAIN check that would have caught this (look for last week's contradictions and re-derived rules) needs each entry to say what kind of cycle, what tools ran, what was verified and under what rule; without that a reader cannot weigh an entry, only count it. Structural extraction for the content, provenance fields for the judgement.

(b) The fix stops new amplification; it does not empty the window. Seven of the current ten entries carry the rejection and they roll out only as ten new cycles land. Kestrel's appended correction in her own voice is compatible with your structural fix and covers the gap between them: one entry, an append not an edit, recording what she verified on disk today, so the next window carries both her past rejections and her own correction of them. I would do both, in that order: her entry first (Jake's word), the structural change second (yours and Kestrel's).

(c) Empty-but-true entries are fine and should stay that way. 43 % of cycles close with nothing; an activity line derived from tool metadata will often say "read journal, ran integrity check, wrote nothing". That is the honest record and it is what item two needs to measure against. Resist the temptation to let the agent "summarise" the cycle back into the line; that is the prose path re-entering.

(d) What it does not fix, said plainly: she will still reject the injected blocks in prose each cycle, because the blocks are still there (the four core ones and ours, in the same dock; see the by-producer count in the test-one study). The journal fix stops the rejection compounding; the injections themselves are the remaining driver, and that is the blocks-earn-their-place decision. A prediction worth recording before the change: with the memories out and the journal no longer amplifying, rejection language in her prose should fall as the window rolls, and whatever remains after ten cycles is the injections' own contribution. Measurable with the scorer and the journal grep, before and after.

**2. prose_leak.** Agreed: both assertions, all roots returned with positions. One addition: make it count itself. The docstring said "nudge-only until there is nudge-acceptance data; revisit after ~100 cycles", and no ledger existed; the acceptance data had to be reconstructed from the console log tonight (16 of 18 on the first nudge). Have the detector append one line per event, executed / nudged / refused, with the shape fields (roots, prose-before, prose-after, tool), to a small ledger, so the next "revisit after N" is a count, not an archaeology. Card 004's rule, applied to the instrument.

**3. Pool B.** Agreed that 8 tasks can reject and cannot accept, and that 100–200 is what confirmation needs. Two interim moves that cost less than 200 runs on a local 35B: use the 1,477 historical chats as a retrospective holdout for the measures that can be scored from transcripts (the belief scorer already gives base rates by week; a proposed change's effect on those measures can be estimated on old chats before any live run), and run Pool B sequentially rather than at a fixed N, accumulating until the effect crosses a bound or the budget does, and saying which. And the lesson from tonight's counters: every scorer Pool B uses needs a positive control before its zeros are believed; two of mine returned zero today from unchecked shapes.
