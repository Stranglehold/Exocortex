### WWII: The Origins of SIGINT

**Bletchley Park** (1939-1945) was Britain's central codebreaking establishment. Key facts:

- **Ultra**: The intelligence derived from breaking German Enigma and Lorenz ciphers. Bletchley Park's team of ~9,000 people (75% women) included mathematicians (Alan Turing), linguists, chess champions, and engineers.
- **Colossus** (1943-1944): The world's first programmable digital electronic computer, built to decrypt Lorenz-encrypted German military communications.
- **ENIAC** connection: The American counterpart, with comparable computing power, was built to decrypt Japanese naval codes.
- **Duration impact**: Historians estimate Ultra shortened the war by 2-4 years and saved ~14 million lives. The contribution was officially classified until the mid-1970s.
- **The Enigma**: German military encryption machine. Bletchley Park's breakthrough was developing the "bombe" — an electromechanical device that could systematically test possible Enigma settings.

**Key insight**: SIGINT's wartime success proved that signals intelligence could decisively influence military outcomes, creating institutional momentum for its post-war expansion.
