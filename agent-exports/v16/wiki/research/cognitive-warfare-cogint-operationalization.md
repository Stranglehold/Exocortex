## Overview

Cognitive warfare and Cognitive Intelligence (COGINT) have transitioned from theoretical concept to operational military doctrine in 2025-2026. NATO Chief Scientist Report 2025 formalized cognitive warfare architecture. Conde & Whiskeyman (Taylor & Francis 2025) proposed COGINT as the sixth intelligence collection discipline alongside SIGINT, HUMINT, GEOINT, OSINT, and MASINT. 2026 NDAA includes specific directive to Secretary of Defense on cognitive warfare countermeasures.

## COGINT as Intelligence Discipline

**Definition (Conde & Whiskeyman, Taylor & Francis 2025):**

Cognitive Intelligence (COGINT) is the systematic mapping, safeguarding, and operational exploitation of decision-making architectures in the contemporary cognitive battlespace. Key distinctions:

- **COGINT ≠ PsyOps/InfoOps** — those are delivery mechanisms. COGINT is the intelligence discipline that maps *what* to target and *how* the adversary thinks.
- **COGINT ≠ OSINT** — OSINT collects open-source data. COGINT analyzes cognitive decision-making structures at scale.
- **COGINT focus:** Understanding adversary cognitive processes, protecting friendly decision-making from manipulation, strategically leveraging cognitive advantage.

## NATO Cognitive Warfare Framework (2025 Report)

**NATO Chief Scientist Research Report on Cognitive Warfare** established the foundational architecture:

1. **Cognitive Warfare Capabilities** — understanding how AI, social media, and emerging technologies enable cognitive domain operations
2. **Cognitive Resilience** — protecting populations and institutions from manipulation, building societal immunity
3. **Whole-of-Society Defense** — critical infrastructure resilience, public trust infrastructure, democratic institution hardening

**NATO House Model:** 7 critical S&T knowledge areas in 3 foundational pillars + 4 cross-cutting force multipliers.

### NATO STO Indicators Framework (STO-MP-HFM-377)

**NATO STO SAS-185** published "Cognitive Warfare Indicators and Warnings Identification Framework" — the first attempt to quantify cognitive attack detection:

- **Purpose**: Develop quantifiable (and/or LLM-enabled) indicators of cognitive attacks and CogWar operations for use in future software solutions
- **Indicator categories**: Information environment shifts, narrative velocity changes, coordination pattern anomalies, sentiment polarization spikes
- **Detection approach**: Baseline establishment → deviation detection → attribution confidence scoring
- **Key finding**: Measurable indicators exist but require continuous baseline recalibration; static thresholds produce high false-positive rates
- **Verification**: NATO STO publication, unclassified

### Deppe & Schaal Conceptual Analysis (2024)

**NATO ACT Cognitive Warfare Exploratory Concept** — Deppe & Schaal (2024) provided the most rigorous conceptual analysis of cognitive warfare to date:

- **Core contribution**: Reduced conceptual ambiguity in cognitive warfare doctrine by establishing necessary vs sufficient conditions for cognitive warfare classification
- **Key distinction**: Cognitive warfare ≠ information warfare; CW targets the *architecture* of belief formation, not just belief content
- **Operationalization requirement**: Without measurable indicators and trained forces, cognitive warfare remains a theoretical concept rather than operational capability
- **Verification**: Published conceptual analysis, NATO ACT framework

### Finnish Defense Forces Research Review (2026-2)

Puolustusvoimat Research Review 2026-2 by Kaarkoski provides systematic comparison of cognitive warfare vs information warfare vs psychological operations. Finnish analysis distinguishes:
- **Information warfare:** targets information flow (jamming, deception)
- **Psychological operations:** targets emotions and morale
- **Cognitive warfare:** targets the decision-making architecture itself — how individuals and groups form beliefs, make choices, and coordinate action

### Dr. James Giordano INSS Analysis (January 2026)

INSS/NDU commentary identifies three core shifts in cognitive warfare doctrine:
1. **From persuasion to architecture** — not changing minds but restructuring how decisions are made
2. **From campaign to continuous** — persistent cognitive environment shaping, not episodic operations
3. **From military to whole-of-society** — targets include civilian institutions, markets, scientific communities

Key operationalization requirement: "initiatives in cognitive engagement become operational capabilities; with measurable indicators, trained forces, partnered resilience, and governance of dual-use S&T."

### NATO CCDCOE Ontological Foundations (2026)

NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence published "Ontological Foundations of Cognitive Warfare" — frames cognitive warfare as a "constitutively invisible" attack on truth, institutional trust, and the fundamental frameworks of discovery. Key contribution: establishes that cognitive warfare targets not just individual cognition but the shared ontological ground that enables collective knowledge.

| Category | Indicator | Detection Method | Cross-Ref |
|----------|-----------|------------------|------------|
