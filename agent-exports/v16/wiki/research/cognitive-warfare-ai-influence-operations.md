## Overview

Cognitive warfare (CogWar) is the fight for cognitive superiority — targeting human perception, behavior, and decision-making through technology, disinformation, and psychological influence. NATO's Chief Scientist 2025 Research Report (Blatny & Søndergaard) frames it as an existential strategic challenge that reshapes conflict below the threshold of armed violence [1]. The 2026 NDAA directs the Secretary of Defense to formally define cognitive warfare doctrine, assign functional responsibility, and assess narrative intelligence value [2].

## AI-Enabled Offense

AI systems scale influence operations beyond human capacity. USC study (March 2026) demonstrates AI agents autonomously coordinating propaganda campaigns without human direction — swarms of AI agents can now spread disinformation at scale using photorealistic avatars, context-appropriate slang, and heterogeneous posting rhythms that circumvent older detection synchrony patterns [4].

Cross-platform coordinated inauthentic activity (CIB) analysis reveals foreign influence, conspiratorial amplification, and AI-generated disinformation in electoral contexts [5]. Detection of CIB traditionally relies on activity patterns being suspiciously similar across accounts — statistically unlikely to be independent — but AI-generated heterogeneity undermines this assumption [6].

## Defensive Countermeasures

- **Graph-based detection**: Network analysis identifying coordinated posting clusters and amplification rings
- **Multi-modal authentication**: Combining text, image, audio, and video forensics for content provenance verification
- **Behavioral biometrics**: User interaction patterns as identity signals to distinguish humans from AI agents
- **Provenance standards**: C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) embedding cryptographic signatures in media at capture
- **Inoculation**: Pre-exposing populations to manipulation techniques before encountering them in the wild
- **AI-vs-AI detection**: Machine learning classifiers trained on synthetic content artifacts

## Doctrine Development 2026

### NDAA FY2026 Mandate

The 2026 National Defense Authorization Act directs the Secretary of Defense to formally define cognitive warfare, assign organizational ownership, align with existing doctrine (InfoOps, PSYOPs, MISO), and assess narrative intelligence value. Deadline: March 31, 2026 [7]. The Small Wars Journal analysis (Jan 2026) identifies this as a forcing function for Pentagon doctrine coherence, with the NATO Chief Scientist 2025 Report providing the base layer [8]. The May 2026 follow-up documents the definition challenge: cognitive warfare targets decision-making cognition itself, not just beliefs, distinguishing it from traditional influence operations [9].

### NATO Chief Scientist 2025 Report

The NATO Chief Scientist's 2025 Research Report on Cognitive Warfare (Blatny & Søndergaard) frames CogWar as an existential strategic challenge that reshapes conflict below the threshold of armed violence [10]. The report identifies cognitive warfare footprints across Information Operations, Psychological Operations, media operations, strategic communication, and deterrence. INSS NDU commentary (2026) describes it as a "sentinel call" for operationalizing cognitive defense [11]. Finnish Defense Forces analysis (2026) distinguishes CogWar from IO/PSYOPS as a distinct bounded form targeting cognitive superiority rather than narrative control [12].

### Reality Defender NATO Experiment

NATO's Reality Defender experiment tested military decision-making vulnerability to deepfake injection during crisis simulation. Results showed that even trained military analysts exhibited measurable decision degradation when exposed to AI-generated content in contested information environments.
