# Semiconductor Supply Chain Geopolitics

## Status: STABLE
## Created: 2026-05-19
## Last Updated: 2026-05-19

## Overview
Analysis of the global semiconductor supply chain from a geopolitical and strategic perspective, focusing on export controls, manufacturing capacity, and technological competition between major powers. As of mid-2026, the US-China semiconductor contest has evolved from targeted AI chip restrictions to broad equipment-based controls, with China pursuing aggressive self-sufficiency targets.

## US Export Control Architecture

### Current Framework (2026)
- **H.R.8287 Semiconductor Controls Effectiveness Act of 2026** — introduced Apr 15, 2026, codifies ongoing export control regime (Congress.gov, 119th Congress)
- **MATCH Act** — proposed Apr 2, 2026; would extend US controls to block China from DUV lithography tools it cannot manufacture domestically; affects South Korean and Taiwanese foundries with Chinese fabs
- **Section 232 Tariffs** — Proclamation 11,002 (Jan 14, 2026) implements 25% tariff component of CHIPS for Security Act
- **AI Diffusion Rule scrapped May 2025** — reversed by new administration; H200 AI processors permitted to China since Dec 2025

### CHIPS Act Implementation
- **Advanced Manufacturing Investment Credit expires end-2026** — bipartisan agreement to extend, per S&P Global Market Intelligence Apr 2026 hearing
- **OIG Report OIG-25-021-I (Jun 2, 2025)** — Commerce CHIPS Act programs status: NIST administering $50B+ over 6 years
- **GAO-25-107386** — Commerce implemented advanced semiconductor controls; structural supply constraints and export control bottlenecks threaten US chip capacity

## Chinese Semiconductor Capabilities

### SMIC Production Status (2026)
- **7nm process demonstrated** — production capability verified despite EUV ban
- **5nm at ~20% yield** — limited production runs, 1.6M dies targeted for 2026
- **DUV lithography loophole** — China using older immersion DUV to print near-frontier AI chips (AEI Apr 2026 report)
- Full 5-10 years behind TSMC's cutting-edge 3nm/2nm

### Self-Sufficiency Targets

### Equipment Dependency
- **ASML EUV remains critical bottleneck** — no domestic alternative exists
- **TrendForce analysis**: China advancing semiconductor equipment self-sufficiency, with EUV as key bottleneck relative to ASML dominance
- **ASML stock impacted** — Apr 7, 2026 US proposed export curbs on China caused share decline

## Strategic Assessment

### What Works for US Controls
- EUV denial remains effective — China cannot build extreme UV lithography tools
- Equipment-based restrictions (vs chip-based) are more durable

### What's Failing
- H200 reversal (Dec 2025) undermines credibility of AI chip restrictions
- DUV multi-patterning allows narrow node advancement despite EUV ban
- Self-sufficiency accelerating faster than anticipated (33%→50% in one year)

### Key Vulnerabilities
1. **Extended timeline risk** — if China reaches 80% self-sufficiency by 2030, US leverage diminishes significantly
2. **Allied coordination gaps** — South Korea and Taiwan have commercial exposure in China
3. **Economic cost** — ASML and other equipment makers face revenue loss from Chinese market exclusion
