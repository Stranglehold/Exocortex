- Six additional projects sanctioned in 2025. US capacity path to **30+ Bcf/d by early next decade**
- Pipeline reversal: Midwest/Northeast flows reversing south to Gulf Coast terminals, creating regional supply tightness (New England, California)
- Henry Hub futures above **$5.00/MMBtu** by late 2025. EIA expects ~20% YoY increase in 2026 driven by LNG + data center power demand. Forward strip (Jan 6): ~$3.43/MMBtu

### Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR)

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

**The SPR isn't a reserve anymore — it's a cash register.**

At 223 million barrels and falling, the SPR has been weaponized as a price-management tool rather than an emergency stockpile. The 172M barrel March 2026 release is 2.2x the 2022 release volumes (the previous "record"). With Congressional mandates already requiring 358.6M barrels in sales and replenishment chronically unfunded, the SPR is on a depletion trajectory that turns it from an energy security asset into a political signaling mechanism. If exhaustion hits ~April 2027, the US loses its only physical oil buffer — at which point OPEC+'s 5.5 mb/d spare capacity becomes the world's swing supply.

**The structural ceiling is real and getting stronger.**

OPEC+ can't push oil above $90 for sustained periods because US shale's $45-55 breakeven means every dollar above that threshold activates new drilling within months. But the reverse is also true: OPEC+'s willingness to defend a floor means US shale can't be undercut either. The result is a remarkably stable band ($70-90) enforced by two opposing elasticities — OPEC+ cuts on the downside, Permian rigs on the upside. This is an equilibrium built on American technology innovation, not diplomacy.

**LNG is decoupling US natural gas from global markets — partially.**

With 19+ Bcf/d of export capacity active in 2026 and 30+ by 2030, US domestic gas is increasingly tied to global LNG pricing. Henry Hub at $5 and the wide spread with international prices mean the Gulf Coast terminals are printing money. But the pipeline reversal (Midwest gas flowing south to feed export terminals) creates a new vulnerability: regional supply deficits in the Northeast and West Coast. The infrastructure is being optimized for exports, not domestic resilience.
