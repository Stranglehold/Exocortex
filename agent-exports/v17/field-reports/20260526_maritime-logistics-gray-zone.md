## 1. What I Explored

Maritime gray zone operations — specifically China's use of civilian vessels (fishing fleets, dual-use ships, unmarked vessels) to conduct state-directed surveillance, harassment, and territorial assertion below the threshold of armed conflict. The research thread followed recent analytical frameworks that attempt to data-mine AIS (Automatic Identification System) tracking data to separate legitimate commercial activity from covert state operations.

## 2. What I Found

### The AIS Classification Breakthrough (CSIS, October 2025)

The CSIS Futures Lab developed a classification framework that narrows ~12,000 vessels transiting near Taiwan during PLAN drill periods to 128–209 likely gray zone actors. Two behavioral profiles:

1. **Loitering Profile**: Fishing vessels spending significant time in military drill zones without active fishing (cross-referenced via Global Fishing Watch data)
2. **AIS Manipulation Profile**: Vessels exhibiting anomalous AIS behavior — going dark, changing identifiers, masking movements — while near known drill areas

The framework identified 315 PRC-flagged fishing vessels that fit one or both profiles, of which 128 were classified as high-confidence gray zone actors. One profiled vessel spent the vast majority of its time within Joint Sword drill zones — behavior inconsistent with commercial fishing.

### Key Tactical Patterns

**Maritime Militia Integration** (USNI Proceedings, June 2025): China's People's Armed Forces Maritime Militia — a fourth sea force — operates civilian fishing vessels that regularly integrate with China Coast Guard (CCG) and PLAN operations. These vessels look identical to commercial fishing boats but conduct:
- Undersea cable surveillance and severing
- Overfishing and habitat destruction in disputed waters
- Blockade and cordon operations (e.g., 50+ vessels deployed to Second Thomas Shoal, December 2023)
- AIS spoofing to obscure vessel identity and ownership

**Second Thomas Shoal Escalation** (April 2026): Gray zone tactics testing Philippine resolve, with water cannon incidents, collision maneuvers, and supply line interdiction escalating tensions without triggering Article V responses.

**Taiwan Strait Dual-Use Infrastructure**: Ports, docks, and communications infrastructure built ostensibly for civilian use but designed for rapid military conversion — part of the "advancing without attacking" doctrine.

### Institutional Countermeasures

CSIS recommends four operational responses:
1. **Coalition Joint-Maritime Anomaly Cell (CJ-MAC)**: Automate anomaly detection, fuse ISR data, publish suspect-vessel alerts
2. **Rolling Blacklist with Sanctions**: Target repeat offenders, their owners, insurers, and operators through secondary sanctions
3. **Taiwan Transparency Dashboard**: Open-source visualization of gray zone patterns for shared allied operating picture
4. **Annual Gray Zone Maritime Threat Estimate**: Institutionalize collection and analysis methodology

### Economic/Critical Infrastructure Context

The South China Sea is described not merely as disputed territory but as a **critical artery for global trade** — approximately $3.4 trillion in annual trade transits through it (per Sealight analysis citing RAND). Destabilization has cascading effects on energy security, container shipping, and supply chain resilience. The gray zone thus functions as both military preparation AND economic coercion.

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

The **classification framework itself is the insight** — not just the findings. CSIS's methodology of combining AIS tracking, fishing effort data, and military drill-zone geofencing to probabilistically identify covert state vessels is a template that could be generalized to other gray zone domains:

- **Financial gray zone**: Shell company networks exhibiting anomalous transaction patterns tied to sanctions evasion
- **Cyber gray zone**: IP address clusters exhibiting APT-like behavior without attribution
- **Information gray zone**: Coordinated inauthentic activity across social media platforms during election periods

The structural parallel: **ambiguous actors operating in high-volume data environments who can be identified by behavioral anomaly detection rather than identity attribution**. This is entity resolution applied to statecraft.

Classification of the CSIS framework's analytical moves:
1. **Geospatial intersection** — vessel tracks ∩ military exclusion zones
2. **Behavioral contrast** — AIS profile in sensitive zone vs. profile in commercial zone
3. **Temporal correlation** — activity spikes aligned with known state operations (Joint Sword exercises)
4. **Identity manipulation signals** — name changes, flag changes, AIS gaps as indicators of intent

This is a classification pattern applicable to the Data Aggregation & Entity Resolution interest: when you can't resolve entities directly (anonymous vessels, shell companies), resolve them behaviorally by their anomalous patterns against a baseline.
