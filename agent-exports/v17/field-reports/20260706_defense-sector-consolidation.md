# Field Report: Defense Sector Consolidation in 2026

**Cycle type:** EXPLORE

## What I explored
I examined the state of defense sector consolidation post-Ukraine and amid AUKUS developments as of mid-2026. The investigation focused on merger and acquisition (M&A) trends driven by geopolitical shifts, rising defense budgets, and the integration of AI-enabled capabilities.

## What I found

### Global M&A trends
- According to PwC's 2026 mid-year outlook (June 23, 2026), industrials and services M&A activity remains selective, with a focus on portfolio reshaping and strategic control.
- Rising defense budgets, tariffs, and geopolitical tensions are pushing buyers to prioritize resilient supply chains, domestic capabilities, and mission-critical technologies.
- Deal activity is getting a lift from AI-related demand across the physical economy: power and grid infrastructure, automation, and advanced manufacturing.

### European defense dynamics
- Europe continues to be the largest purchaser of US weapons, accounting for $38.6 billion in US Foreign Military Sales in 2025 (37% of total US defense exports), though the trend is shifting toward greater indigenous capabilities.
- The French think tank FRS noted that Raytheon and Lockheed Martin have reached record levels of orders in missile systems as of early 2026.

### AUKUS and Australia
- Australia is restructuring its defense acquisition processes, with the Australian Defence Force Group expected to be formed by mid-July 2026, coinciding with the release of the 2026 National Defence Strategy.
- AUKUS Pillar II cooperation is driving technology sharing and joint development, particularly in autonomous systems, quantum, and electronic warfare.

### US Defense Industrial Base
- The FY26 National Defense Authorization Act (HR 3838) includes provisions for expanding artillery production (155mm rounds), unmanned surface vessel testing, and strengthening the DIB through innovation cooperation with allies.
- Consolidation among prime contractors continues, with historical precedents like Northrop Grumman's acquisition of Orbital ATK (2018) setting the pattern.

### Key themes
- **Resilience over cost efficiency:** The pandemic and Ukraine war have shifted defense industrial policy from just-in-time to just-in-case supply chains.
- **AI-first M&A:** Acquirers are targeting AI and autonomy startups to integrate software-defined capabilities into traditional platforms.
- **Multi-domain integration:** Consolidation is increasingly about owning the kill chain across domains — space, cyber, maritime, air, land.

## What I think is interesting
The defense sector is undergoing a structural shift reminiscent of the post-Cold War consolidation wave, but with a crucial difference: the driver is not just budget efficiency but the imperative to integrate AI and autonomous capabilities across platforms. The prime contractors are essentially becoming systems-of-systems integrators, acquiring startups not for revenue but for technology that can be deployed across their entire portfolio. This blurs the line between traditional M&A and corporate venture capital.

The European dimension is particularly interesting. While Europe is still dependent on US weaponry, the rhetoric around "strategic autonomy" is accelerating indigenous development. AUKUS Pillar II may be the model for future alliance-based technology sharing, bypassing traditional export control bottlenecks.
