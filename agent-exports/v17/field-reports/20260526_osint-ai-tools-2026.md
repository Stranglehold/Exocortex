## 2. What I found

### The paradigm shift: from aggregation to agentic investigation

2026 marks a categorical change: AI is not merely augmenting OSINT; it is restructuring the investigative workflow. A new class of tools doesn't just surface data — it reasons, simulates, and predicts, behaving like a team of analysts.

### Key new tools

- **MiroFish** (March 2026, ~40K GitHub stars, backed by Shanda Group): Takes a seed document (news article, intelligence report) and turns it into a simulated parallel world. Extracts entities, builds a GraphRAG knowledge graph, and spawns thousands of autonomous agents with unique personalities to simulate how a scenario evolves across society. Use case: geopolitical threat modeling, forecasting.

- **GeoSeer** (late 2025): Parallel multi-agent AI that analyzes raw visual cues (landmarks, architecture, terrain, signage, vegetation) and returns GPS coordinates with street-level accuracy — without EXIF metadata. Use case: geolocating protest footage, verifying origin of surveillance stills.

- **OSINT Industries**: Real-time identity resolution for law enforcement. Claims 5,000+ departments worldwide. Resolves email addresses, phone numbers, and linked accounts across social media and financial apps.

### Incumbent responses

- **Maltego**: Added browser-based AI Assistant for person-of-interest investigations and real-time social media sentiment analysis.
- **Shodan**: Integrated AI-based threat predictions in 2025.
- **Palantir**: Remains a big-data analytics platform for multi-source fusion in government/military contexts.

### Top 10 tools (from Hackread review)

## 3. What I think is interesting

- **The tension between capability and friction.** Tools are more powerful than ever, but the operational environment (API restrictions, bot detection, rate limits) is more hostile than ever. The market sells capability; practitioners buy friction.

- **The agentic leap is qualitative, not incremental.** MiroFish represents a shift from "what happened?" to "what could happen next?" — a simulation engine, not just a search engine. This is analogous to the shift from rule-based expert systems to generative AI, applied to intelligence analysis.

- **The regulatory asymmetry is a strategic variable.** If the EU enforces the AI Act rigorously from August 2026, it may create a structural disadvantage for European law enforcement relative to peers in less regulated jurisdictions. Conversely, it may become a defacto standard that shapes global OSINT tool design.

- **Provenance is the unsolved problem.** No current tool cryptographically proves that gathered OSINT is authentic and unmodified. This is a gap that intersects directly with verifiable credentials, zero-knowledge proofs, and the privacy/cryptography interest area.
