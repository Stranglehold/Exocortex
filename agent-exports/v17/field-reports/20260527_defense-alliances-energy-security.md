## 2. What I Found

### Regional Defense Alliance Fragmentation

**The core shift:** Traditional US-led security guarantees are being reassessed globally. The Middle East conflict exposed three simultaneous dynamics:

1. **GCC recalibration:** Gulf Cooperation Council members are expanding purchases of US defense systems but simultaneously exploring collective security arrangements, European partnerships, and hedging with China/Russia. Some GCC states are granting the US full base access to raise deterrent costs for Iran, but this is transactional, not structural alignment.

2. **European strategic autonomy struggles:** EU defense expenditure reached €381B in 2025, exceeding the 2% GDP target, and NATO raised its goal to 3.5% by 2035. However, fiscal constraints and domestic resistance to social program cuts make "strategic autonomy" difficult. The emerging pattern: leading European states may seek to transform NATO into a European-led regional defense organization while expanding defense-industrial cooperation with Japan, India, South Korea, and Gulf states.

3. **Asia-Pacific diversification:** The Shangri-La Dialogue (May 29-31 2026) will be the key venue for assessing US security policy impacts. The Philippines-Japan Reciprocal Access Agreement signals increasing bilateral defense arrangements outside US-led frameworks. India's partnerships with Israel (joint tech development) and Cyprus (maritime intel sharing) demonstrate strategic autonomy while remaining aligned with US architecture. South Korea: 76% public support for indigenous nuclear capabilities.

**Key numbers:**
- Japan reached 2% GDP defense target in 2025 — two years early
- EU defense spend: €381B (2025), exceeding NATO's 2% target
- NATO new target: 3.5% of GDP by 2035
- South Korea: 76% public support for domestic nuclear weapons

### Energy Security: The "Electrostate" Thesis

The Strait of Hormuz disruption (~20% of global LNG supply shut down) is driving a structural pivot from fuel security to electricity system security. The IEA describes it as the largest supply disruption in global oil market history.

**The "electrostate" concept (EY terminology):** Countries building domestic power grids, storage, and electrified transport are better positioned to absorb fuel-supply shocks. Electrification becomes an energy-security hedge, not just a decarbonization pathway.

**Vulnerability dynamics:**
- Asia most exposed: ~90% of Hormuz LNG volumes went to Asia in 2025
- Europe exposed through price linkage (TTF gas benchmark +60% in March 2026)
- US EIA base case: Hormuz traffic doesn't return to pre-conflict levels until late 2026

**New risk concentrations:** As economies electrify, vulnerability shifts from tankers/pipelines to grids, cyber systems, critical-mineral processing (aluminum, sulfur-linked chemicals), and power-electronics supply chains.

**Policy response:** IEA coordinated largest-ever emergency oil stock release. EU, UK, India, Japan activated fuel security, storage, and consumer protection measures.

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

Three cross-cutting dynamics make this moment historically significant:

**First — the defense-energy nexus is becoming explicit.** National security strategy is converging with energy policy in ways not seen since the 1970s oil shocks. The "electrostate" thesis means countries are making defense-motivated energy investments: domestic generation, grid hardening, storage. This blurs the line between energy policy and defense procurement.

**Second — alliance architecture is fragmenting, not splitting.** The pattern isn't a clean break from US leadership but a proliferation of overlapping, sometimes competing security arrangements. States are stacking alliances: staying in NATO while building bilateral defense-industrial ties, maintaining US basing agreements while exploring GCC collective security. This creates a more complex, less predictable security environment where escalation risks multiply.

**Third — the critical mineral connection completes the triangle.** This cycle's defense/energy findings connect directly to the prior cycle's semiconductor/rare earth analysis. The three chokepoints — advanced semiconductors, rare earth processing, and energy transit (Hormuz) — are mutually reinforcing. Disruption in one amplifies vulnerability in the others. A country dependent on imported semiconductors, imported rare earths, and imported LNG is triply exposed.
