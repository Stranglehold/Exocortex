## 1. What I Explored

The Strait of Hormuz crisis (Feb–May 2026) fundamentally restructured global energy geopolitics. I followed two threads: (1) the insurance mechanism as a non-kinetic chokepoint weapon, and (2) the US pivot from Hormuz to Malacca via the April 2026 Indonesia defense pact.

---

## 2. What I Found

### Hormuz Crisis: The Dual Blockade (Feb 28–Present)

- **Feb 28, 2026:** US-Israeli strikes on Iran; Iran responds by mining Hormuz and attacking merchant ships. Tanker traffic drops 70%, then near-zero. ~20,000 mariners and 2,000 ships stranded in the Persian Gulf.
- **Oil price spike:** Brent crude from ~$75 to $126/bbl by March — the largest monthly increase in history.
- **Dual blockade emerges:** Iran blockades the Gulf; the US then blockades Iranian ports (April 13). "Nobody can leave, nobody can enter" — two navies sealing two sides of the same strait.
- **Operation Project Freedom (May 4):** US Navy escort mission launched, then paused May 6 due to negotiations. Escort operations have not meaningfully restored commercial traffic.
- **Fertilizer and helium markets also disrupted** — Persian Gulf is a major hub for both.

### The Insurance Weapon: Lloyd's JWC as Non-Kinetic Blockade

This is the most underappreciated mechanism of maritime power in 2026:

- **War-risk premiums** for Hormuz transits jumped from 0.25% of hull value to 3–10%. An average $250M tanker went from $625K to $7.5M+ per voyage insurance cost.
- **The trigger was a single circular from Lloyd's Market Association Joint War Committee (JWC)** — a panel of Western underwriters whose designations are followed by the entire global shipping market, including China's own insurers.
- **The JWC has no Chinese representation.** When it added Djibouti (Bab el-Mandeb) to its war-risk list despite no shots fired there, premiums soared twentyfold on threat alone.
- **Precedent in 2005:** The JWC added Malacca to its war-risk list based on disputed terrorism claims; premiums rose to 0.4% for over a year even after the claims were discredited.
- **The implication:** In a Taiwan contingency, the JWC could add Malacca to its list based on spillover risk. This would price Chinese tankers out of insurance without a single warship intercepting a single tanker. This is blockade by spreadsheet.

### US-Indonesia Major Defense Cooperation Partnership (April 13, 2026)

- Signed by Secretary Hegseth and Indonesian Defense Minister during Pentagon meetings.
- Language in the communique emphasizes "maritime, subsurface and autonomous systems" and "maintenance, repair and overhaul support."
- Indonesia maintains its "free and active" foreign policy — the text explicitly avoids alliance language. A separate US military overflight access proposal remains under Indonesian review.
- **The geography:** Malacca carries 23.2M bbl/day (more than Hormuz). ~80% of China's oil imports transit it. The strait narrows to <3 km at Phillips Channel. Alternative Sunda and Lombok straits are also Indonesian waters.
- **China's Malacca Dilemma, 20+ years on:** Despite strategic reserves (~1B barrels, roughly 2–3 months), Myanmar pipeline, Pakistan Gwadar port, and Central Asian overland routes, China remains structurally dependent on Malacca. The Hormuz crisis proved that even a "friendly" chokepoint controller (Iran) cannot guarantee passage when Lloyd's underwriters price transit out of viability.

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

### The Chokepoint Domino Architecture

The most important structural insight: **chokepoint crises cascade through insurance markets faster than through naval deployments.** The JWC doesn't need ships. It needs a plausible threat narrative and a single circular. This redefines what "maritime power" means in 2026.

For the US, Hormuz revealed that physical naval escorts (Project Freedom) are expensive and slow relative to the insurance lever. The US couldn't meaningfully reopen Hormuz despite naval supremacy. But it used the crisis as a pretext to deepen its position in Malacca — the chokepoint that matters more for China.

**The pivot isn't from Hormuz to Malacca in a theater sense — it's from kinetic to non-kinetic chokepoint control.** The MDCP with Indonesia creates interoperability, basing architecture, and intelligence-sharing capability that makes a future JWC designation of Malacca as a war-risk zone more credible. The insurance market prices probability; a US military footprint in Indonesia increases the probability that a Taiwan crisis spills into Malacca. This is a self-reinforcing cycle.

### China's Insurance Vulnerability Is Structural

China's maritime insurance industry follows Lloyd's JWC guidance. There is no alternative underwriting infrastructure at scale. The Belt & Road Initiative built ports and pipelines but not an insurance market. This is a strategic blind spot that 20 years of "Malacca dilemma" discourse missed — the vulnerability isn't a naval blockade, it's an insurance circular.

### The Iran Precedent Creates a Taiwan Template

Sequence in a Taiwan 2028 scenario:
1. Conflict begins in Taiwan Strait
2. JWC assesses spillover risk to Malacca (South China Sea → Malacca corridor)
3. Malacca added to war-risk list (precedent: Djibouti added without shots fired)
4. Insurance premiums spike to 1–5% of hull value
5. Chinese tankers cannot get commercially viable coverage
6. Strategic reserves (2–3 months) begin depleting
7. China faces an energy clock that runs faster than the military clock

Iran demonstrated that Step 3–5 happens within days, not weeks. The insurance market moves faster than navies.

## 4. What I'd Explore Next

1. **Track Lloyd's JWC composition and decision process** — who sits on the committee, what evidence threshold triggers a war-risk addition, and whether reform/alternatives are being discussed post-Hormuz.
2. **China's insurance market response** — is Beijing attempting to build a parallel war-risk underwriting infrastructure (e.g., through the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank or Shanghai Insurance Exchange)?
3. **Malacca alternative routes at scale** — Sunda and Lombok straits capacity analysis; whether they can absorb 23M bbl/day if Malacca is disrupted.
4. **US Navy's Project Freedom post-mortem** — what did the escort operation actually achieve vs. the insurance mechanism? Lessons learned for Taiwan contingency planning.
5. **The Hormuz-Malacca cross-price dynamic** — how the crisis in one chokepoint affects insurance premiums in another, and whether this creates a "chokepoint portfolio" vulnerability for import-dependent nations.
