## 2. What I Found

### 2.1 Strait of Hormuz Crisis — Supply Shock
- The semi-closure of the Strait of Hormuz (following late-Feb 2026 US-Israel-Iran escalation) caused the largest disruption in global oil flows in history, with supply losses exceeding **10 million barrels per day** during the worst weeks.
- Brent crude climbed above **$114/bbl** in early May 2026, WTI above $106/bbl, with some Asian spot premiums much higher due to shipping scarcity.
- The IEA warned that both supply growth and demand growth would weaken through 2026 as high prices induced "demand destruction."

### 2.2 US Domestic Response — SPR Deficit & DPA Drilling
- The Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen to **~392 million barrels**, the lowest in decades, following emergency releases of 172 million barrels over the fiscal cycle.
- In response, the White House invoked the **Defense Production Act (DPA)** to accelerate drilling under military installations — a "base-level energy" strategy to bypass volatile spot markets.
- Barksdale Air Force Base in Louisiana serves as a precedent; the administration intends to scale this model across oil-rich bases (Permian Basin, Bakken).
- The Bureau of Land Management approved nearly 6,000 oil and gas permits on federal and Native lands, up 55% from prior years.
- A complex exchange arrangement is projected to return ~200 million barrels to the SPR over 12 months via "borrow + interest" from prior emergency loans.

### 2.3 OPEC & US Shale Dynamics
- OPEC forecasts a **110,000 bpd decline** in US crude and condensate production in 2026, citing long-term shale weakening.
- Globally, oversupply was 2.2 mb/d in 2025, expected to widen to 2.3 mb/d in 2026 (Gabelli), driven by long-cycle projects in Brazil and Guyana.
- Yet geopolitical tensions keep prices elevated despite the structural surplus, creating a decoupling between supply/demand fundamentals and fear premiums.

### 2.4 Emerging-Market Vulnerability — Pakistan
- Pakistan's monthly oil import bill surged from ~$300M to nearly **$800M**, a 167% increase that threatens its balance-of-payments, fuel subsidies, and food inflation (via diesel-dependent agricultural logistics).
- This exemplifies how middle powers with high energy dependency are disproportionately impacted by maritime chokepoint disruptions.

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

The invocation of the **Defense Production Act for energy extraction** is a significant shift in the US national security posture. Traditionally, the DPA is reserved for wartime industrial mobilization (tanks, jets, semiconductors post-COVID). Applying it to drill for oil under military bases reframes energy as a direct defense asset, potentially accelerating a permanent decoupling of US energy policy from global market volatility.

Additionally, the simultaneous existence of a global oversupply (2.3 mb/d) and $114/bbl prices is a textbook example of **geopolitical risk premium** overpowering supply-demand fundamentals. This illustrates how chokepoint control (Strait of Hormuz) can create a price floor independent of output capacity.

Key insight: **The US is moving from "consumer of last resort" (SPR release) to "producer of last resort" (DPA-enabled federal drilling)**, which could alter OPEC+ dynamics permanently.
