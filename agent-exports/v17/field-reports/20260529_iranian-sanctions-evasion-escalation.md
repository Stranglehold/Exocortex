## 2. What I Found

### 2.1 The Blockade: From Financial Pressure to Physical Interdiction

On **April 13, 2026**, the U.S. imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports. This marked a qualitative escalation — from designating entities on paper to physically stopping vessels at sea.

**Chronology of interdictions:**

| Date | Event | Location | Significance |
|------|-------|----------|-------------|
| April 13 | Blockade of Iranian ports announced | Gulf of Oman / Strait of Hormuz | First physical trade interdiction since WWII-era blockades |
| April 20 | USS New Orleans (LPD-18) escorts MV Touska | Arabian Sea | Initial enforcement posture |
| ~April 21 | M/T Tifani interdicted | Indo-Pacific Command AOR | First boarding outside Middle East — global reach |
| April 23 | M/T Majestic X boarded and seized | Indian Ocean | Second tanker seizure; Guyana-flagged, stateless |
| April 23 | USS destroyers escort Dorena and Sevin | Departed Chabahar pre-blockade | Pursuit of pre-blockade departures |

**Key detail:** The Majestic X boarding involved "armed American sailors" climbing aboard in the Indian Ocean — far from Hormuz. This signals that enforcement is not limited to chokepoints.

**Scale context:** USNI News reported a record fleet of 171 crude tankers heading toward the U.S. Gulf, while 732 energy carriers waited near the Strait of Hormuz. Shipping patterns shifted to circumnavigating Africa.

### 2.2 Treasury's Network-Based Designations: April 24, 2026

On April 24, 2026, OFAC executed "Economic Fury" — its most structurally significant action against Iranian oil networks:

- **Hengli Petrochemical (Dalian) Refinery Co., Ltd.** — China's second-largest "teapot" (independent) refinery — was directly designated. This is unprecedented: prior teapot designations targeted four smaller refineries; Hengli is a billion-dollar-volume customer.
- **Hengli purchased Iranian crude from Sepehr Energy Jahan Nama Pars Company** — the oil sales arm of Iran's Armed Forces General Staff — generating hundreds of millions in military revenue.
- **19 named vessels** designated, spanning flags: Panama (7), Hong Kong (3), Comoros (2), Barbados (2), Vanuatu (1), Marshall Islands (1), Cook Islands (1), Antigua and Barbuda (1), plus one unknown-flagged.
- **20 shipping companies** designated across jurisdictions: Hong Kong (7), Marshall Islands (4), Panama (2), UAE (1), Vietnam (1), British Virgin Islands (1), China (2), Cayman Islands (1), Liberia (1).
- **Since February 2025**, OFAC has sanctioned over **1,000** Iran-related persons, vessels, and aircraft.

The total volume affected by this single action exceeds **30+ million barrels** of Iranian oil from the 19 vessels.

### 2.3 The China Connection: Teapot Refineries as Strategic Chokepoint

Chinese teapot refineries (independent, non-state-owned) purchase the majority of Iranian crude. By finally designating a major teapot, Treasury is attacking the demand side — not just the supply/maritime transport layer.

**Why this matters:**
- China absorbs ~49% of Russian crude and an estimated 80-90% of Iranian exports
- Hengli had been receiving Iranian cargoes since at least 2023 from already-sanctioned vessels (BIG MAG, GALE, ARES)
- The refinery designation exposes the entire value chain: Iranian military oil sales arm → shadow fleet → Chinese teapot → refined products market

### 2.4 Shadow Fleet Evolution: The Windward Data Picture

The February 25, 2026 designations (30+ entities) and Windward's analysis reveal the shadow fleet's current state:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total tankers engaged in Iranian trade | ~430 |
| Falsely flagged vessels | 62% |
| Sanctioned vessels | 87% |
| Top flag jurisdictions | Marshall Islands, Hong Kong, China, Panama |
| Insurance coverage known | Very few (<10%) |
| LPG carriers in Iranian trades | ~100 (expanding beyond crude) |

**Evasion techniques:**
1. **Flag-hopping** — vessels register in low-oversight jurisdictions (Comoros, Vanuatu, Cook Islands, Barbados)
2. **AIS manipulation** — spoofing, fake IMO/MMSI numbers, deliberate tracking data manipulation
3. **Ship-to-ship transfers** — 3-4 transfers between load and discharge ports to obscure origin
4. **Multi-year vessel reuse** — same hulls operating since 2023-2025 despite prior designations
5. **Opaque ownership chains** — Hong Kong, BVI, Marshall Islands shell companies with no beneficial ownership transparency

### 2.5 The Revenue-to-Weapons Pipeline

Treasury's February 2026 action explicitly linked maritime designations to weapons procurement networks:

- Shahed-series UAV engine production
- Sodium perchlorate procurement (missile propellant precursor)
- IRGC and MODAFL intermediaries

This is the strategic logic: oil exports → revenue → weapons. By designating both transport and procurement simultaneously, Treasury aims to break the closed loop.

### 2.6 Iranian Counter-Response: Tit-for-Tat Interdictions

CBS News reported "tit-for-tat series" of boardings. Iran is also interdicting ships. USNI News described Middle East shipping as "paralyzed" by dueling blockades. This raises the risk of miscalculation — two navies stopping commercial vessels in overlapping waters.

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

### The Escalation Ladder Is Accelerating Faster Than Precedent

In 2 months (February–April 2026), the sanctions enforcement posture shifted from:

1. **Designations** (February 2026: 30+ entities designated)  
   → 2. **Customer sanctions** (April 24: Hengli, a major Chinese refinery)  
   → 3. **Physical blockade** (April 13: port closure)  
   → 4. **High-seas interdiction** (April 21-23: two vessels boarded outside the Gulf)

Each rung of this ladder was historically months or years apart. The compression into weeks suggests either (a) the initial designations were deliberately sequenced as justification for military action, or (b) the Iranians accelerated evasion faster than Treasury could keep up, forcing physical escalation.

### The Chinese Teapot Designation Breaks a Taboo

Until now, Treasury avoided directly designating major Chinese refineries — the diplomatic cost was too high. Hengli's designation signals a willingness to impose secondary sanctions risk on any entity in the Iranian oil value chain, including state-adjacent Chinese firms. This fundamentally changes the risk calculus for every teapot operator and their financiers.

### The Revenue Weapoxification Loop Is Structural, Not Transactional

Windward's data shows ~430 vessels in the ecosystem, 87% already sanctioned, yet the trade continues. This suggests sanctions are not eliminating capacity — they're simply raising the cost (and risk premium) for participants. The shadow fleet isn't shrinking; it's evolving. New flags (Cook Islands, Barbados) replace those that face enforcement.

## 4. What I'd Explore Next

1. **Insurance as the true chokepoint:** Windward notes insurance coverage is "unknown for all but a small handful." Who insures these shadow vessels? If P&I clubs have quietly withdrawn and alternative insurers have emerged (Russian, Chinese, Iranian state-backed), tracking that market would reveal the financial backbone.

2. **Ship-to-ship transfer hubs:** Where do the 3-4 transfers actually occur? Likely locations: Malaysian coast, UAE waters, Singapore Strait. Mapping these transfer nodes could identify chokepoints for interdiction.

3. **Chinese government response to Hengli:** Does Beijing defend Hengli publicly? Does it retaliate against U.S. firms? The Hengli test case will signal whether secondary sanctions can actually deter Chinese corporate behavior.

4. **Dorena and Sevin pursuit:** These vessels departed Chabahar before the blockade and are being escorted by U.S. Navy destroyers. Their fate will set the precedent for pre-blockade departures.

5. **The LPG dimension:** Windward identifies ~100 LPG carriers in Iranian trades — a sector less scrutinized than crude. Iranian LPG exports may be the next vector for evasion.
