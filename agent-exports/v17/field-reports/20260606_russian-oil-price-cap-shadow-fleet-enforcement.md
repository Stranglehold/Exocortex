The thread led through the EU's 21st sanctions package (finalized June 2, 2026), the Atlantic Council's analysis of expiring US sanctions waivers on Russian and Iranian oil, the February 2026 ShipUniverse maritime sanctions roundup, and cross-referenced against the Exocortex wiki's existing STABLE page on Russian oil price cap enforcement.

## 2. What I Found

### EU 21st Package — The LNG Circuit Breaker (June 2, 2026)
- **Price cap frozen at $60/bbl** — rejected a proposed increase to $65 floated earlier in 2026. Rationale: Iran-Israel conflict oil price volatility means any Russian windfall from energy market disruption should be absorbed by the cap, not passed through to Moscow's budget.
- **First-ever LNG restrictions:** After over a year of resistance from Belgium, France, and Spain (all have long-term Russian LNG contracts), the 21st package includes:
  - Ban on LNG re-exports through EU ports to third countries (cuts off transshipment hub usage)
  - Timeline for winding down existing supply contracts
- **20+ additional shadow fleet vessels blacklisted** — bringing total EU-designated shadow fleet to well over 300 vessels denied EU ports, insurance, and maritime services.
- **French physical seizure:** France intercepted and seized a shadow fleet tanker operating under a flag of convenience in the Bay of Biscay off Brittany on June 1, 2026. This is the most high-profile enforcement action under the expanded framework and occurred before the 21st package was even formally adopted.

### The Fractured Coalition (Atlantic Council, April 2026)
- **US general licenses expiring:** Treasury issued licenses (March 2026) allowing Russian and Iranian oil loaded onto tankers as of March 12 into global markets, intended to offset Hormuz disruption supply losses. These expire April 11 (Russia) and April 19 (Iran).
- **The licenses aren't working as intended:** Oil prices continued climbing for three weeks after issuance. Supply relief from Russian/Iranian crude has failed to offset Middle Eastern disruption.
- **Windfall to adversaries:** Russia is reportedly earning an extra $150 million in budget revenue per day; Iran ~$139 million/day (Bloomberg estimates).
- **Policy incoherence:** The US general license effectively superseded the price cap, easing restrictions on Russian oil majors (Lukoil, Rosneft sanctioned October 2025) AND the price cap itself. Yet there was no structural reason to suspend the cap — maintaining it wouldn't have disrupted Russian supply flows.
- **G7 divergence:** European allies lowered the cap to $47 then $44, but the US did not follow, creating enforcement fragmentation.
- **Atlantic Council recommendation:** Reimpose and enforce the price cap on Russian oil while re-introducing an escrow account mechanism for Iranian oil. Use the cap as an oil inflation dampener (ceil what Asian buyers pay) rather than relying solely on supply-side adjustments.

### Shadow Fleet Enforcement Escalation (Feb-June 2026)
- **Three enforcement themes** from ShipUniverse analysis:
  1. More named vessels tied to shadow trade (300+ EU-listed, growing)
  2. Broader maritime services restrictions (not just insurance — port access, bunkering, crewing, classification)
  3. Operational pressure through physical seizures, boardings, and "safety offload" authorizations when environmental risk and sanctions exposure collide
- **French June 1 seizure** represents a threshold moment: diplomatic listing authority combined with physical maritime enforcement. Pre-2026, EU sanctions enforcement was almost entirely paper-based (attestation compliance, financial restrictions). The physical seizure marks escalation from regulatory to operational.
- **Karimun Oil Terminal (Indonesia) designated** in the 20th package (April 2026) — first third-country port listed for shadow fleet connections, alongside Russian ports Murmansk and Tuapse.

### Persistent Structural Vulnerabilities
From the existing Exocortex wiki analysis (which remains accurate):
1. **Insurance attestation fraud** is the core vulnerability — Western insurers/reinsurers rely solely on attestations from shell-company intermediaries in UAE/Hong Kong with no access to actual payment flows.
2. **AIS manipulation** (going dark, position spoofing) continues. Detection requires satellite imagery (SAR/optical) for real-time verification — an asymmetric cost burden on enforcers.
3. **Ship-to-ship transfers** sever traceable custody chains. Common anchorages: Port Said, Malta, Ceuta, Atlantic approaches.
4. **Flag-hopping and corporate obfuscation** — frequent changes of vessel flag, name, and registered owner through multi-jurisdictional shell structures.

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

**The French seizure is a signaling inflection point, not an enforcement solution.** Seizing one tanker out of 300+ shadow fleet vessels is a demonstration of capability, not a deterrent architecture. The shadow fleet is estimated at 600-1,200 vessels depending on classification definition. Even with 300+ EU-designated, the operational reach of physical maritime enforcement against that scale is negligible. The seizure matters because it demonstrates that physical enforcement is now on the table — but absent sustained, multi-lateral operational capacity, it functions as diplomatic theater rather than effective sanctions enforcement.

**The coalition is fragmenting at the worst possible moment.** The US general license + non-adoption of $47/$44 cap levels + European physical enforcement escalation create a three-way policy incoherence: US is providing revenue relief, Europe is tightening restrictions, and the market is arbitraging between them. This is structurally isomorphic to the stovepiping problem in intelligence failure analysis (Ames/Hanssen era) — different agencies apply different rules to the same target, and the target exploits the gaps.

**LNG sanctions are a genuine circuit breaker — but the timeline is soft.** Banning re-exports through EU ports hits Russia's ability to use European infrastructure as a transshipment hub (Novatek Yamal LNG historically transshipped ~20% of cargoes through Zeebrugge and Montoir). But the "timeline for winding down existing contracts" signals political compromise, not operational urgency. Belgium, France, and Spain have long-term contracts that won't vanish overnight. The LNG provision is more important as a precedent (first-ever EU Russian LNG restrictions) than as an immediate revenue impact.

**Attestation fraud is an entity resolution problem.** The core enforcement vulnerability — shell company intermediaries producing fraudulent price attestations — is fundamentally an entity resolution challenge: connect opaque UAE/Hong Kong trading desks to ultimate beneficial owners, link them to sanctioned Russian entities through corporate registries and trade data, then create actionable enforcement targets. This is the same Fellegi-Sunter problem that has emerged as the universal bottleneck across Exocortex research domains (FEC↔LDA campaign finance-to-lobbying resolution, GRIP awardee-to-PUC filer resolution, cross-jurisdictional entity resolution).

## 4. What I'd Explore Next

1. **Shadow fleet ownership chain mapping:** Use IHS Maritime, Equasis, and open shipping registries to trace beneficial ownership of recently designated shadow fleet vessels. Cross-reference with Russian corporate registries and sanctions designation lists. This is a tangible entity resolution project.
2. **Insurance attestation forensic analysis:** Investigate whether any Western insurers/reinsurers have been penalized for accepting fraudulent attestations. The compliance regime appears to depend entirely on self-certification by intermediaries with zero verification — mapping the liability gap would be valuable.
3. **Physical enforcement capability assessment:** What is the EU's actual maritime interdiction capacity in the Mediterranean/Atlantic? The French seizure involved a vessel in the Bay of Biscay — a permissive environment. Shadow fleet activity concentrates off Port Said (Egypt), Malta, and Ceuta — how many of those anchorages fall within practical EU/NATO interdiction range?
4. **LNG contract unwind timeline:** Map Belgian, French, and Spanish long-term Russian LNG contracts — volumes, expiry dates, replacement capacity (US Gulf Coast, Qatar, Australian spot cargoes). Understand the actual revenue disruption timeline.
5. **Coalition realignment prospects:** With US general licenses expiring April 11/19, does Treasury extend or allow lapse? The Atlantic Council argued for reimposition — what is the actual Treasury posture as of June 2026?
