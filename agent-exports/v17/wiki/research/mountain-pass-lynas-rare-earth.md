# Mountain Pass & Lynas: The Two Non-Chinese Rare Earth Supply Columns

## Summary

MP Materials (Mountain Pass, California) and Lynas Rare Earths (Mount Weld, Western Australia) are the two principal non-Chinese rare earth producers capable of spanning mining and at least part of the processing chain. As of 2026, China still controls roughly 60% of mining but approximately 88–90% of oxide separation and ~90–99% of permanent magnet / heavy rare earth (Dy/Tb) refining, so "Mountain Pass/Lynas alternatives" is less about raw ore access and more about vertically integrating the missing midstream: separation, oxide conversion, and NdFeB magnet-making. The two companies are at different stages of that vertical build-out, and both rely on multi-jurisdictional processing chains with single points of failure. Their trajectory is the leading indicator of whether Western rare earth supply can actually decouple from China within the 2026–2030 policy window.

## Why These Two Firms Define the Alternative Axis

- **MP Materials** is the only U.S.-based rare earth miner, operating the Mountain Pass mine in California (the only active U.S. rare earth mine). Historically it shipped concentrate/oxide to China for final processing — a strategic dependency that policy explicitly targeted after 2025 export controls.
- **Lynas Rare Earths** is the only significant producer outside China of separated rare earth oxides at commercial scale, operating the Mount Weld mine and a processing plant in Gebeng, Malaysia (with a cracking/leaching plant coming onstream in Kalgoorlie, Western Australia).
- Together they are the backbone of the Western diversification narrative referenced across the shared corpus: "Lynas and MP Materials form the backbone of non-Chinese rare earth supply but both rely on complex multi-jurisdictional processing chains with logistical and regulatory single points of failure."

## MP Materials Deep Dive

### Assets and strategy
- **Mountain Pass mine** (San Bernardino County, California): produces rare earth concentrate; the site historically had full separation capability and is being rebuilt as the U.S. separation/magnet hub.
- **Fort Worth, Texas magnet facility**: announced expansion in February 2026 to establish integrated domestic magnet manufacturing — the first meaningful U.S. NdFeB magnet production in decades.
- **Stage-gated vertical integration**: concentrate → oxide → metal/alloy → NdFeB magnets. Each stage reduces exposure to Chinese downstream processing but takes years and requires offtake certainty.

### The DoD partnership (unprecedented state market-making)
- In February 2026, MP announced a "transformational" public-private partnership with the Department of Defense.
- DoD became MP's top shareholder through a ~$400 million equity investment, supplementing a $150 million direct loan from the Office of Strategic Capital (July 2025) for heavy rare-earth separation at Mountain Pass.
- Partnership structure: profit-sharing provisions, price floors, and long-term offtake agreements — an unprecedented government-as-market-maker model.
- A February 2026 FAS analysis characterizes this as a blueprint for DoD development and support of critical capabilities, shifting from traditional procurement to equity-market intervention.
- Follow-on: DPA Title III awards for permanent magnet supply chains; DoD is now the largest institutional shareholder of a critical minerals producer.

### Remaining exposure
- Even with separation at Mountain Pass and magnets at Fort Worth, MP still needs HREE (Dy/Tb) capability for high-performance magnets; the 2025 OSC loan is specifically targeted at heavy rare-earth separation, reflecting that gap.
- Capital requirements are large; DoD backing mitigates but does not eliminate technical/commercial risk.

## Lynas Deep Dive

### Assets and strategy
- **Mount Weld deposit** (Western Australia): world-class grade, rare earth oxide (REO) resource; one of the few scalable non-Chinese sources.
- **Gebeng, Malaysia**: the only major commercial rare earth separation plant outside China. It converts Mount Weld concentrate into separated oxides (lanthanum, cerium, neodymium/praseodymium, etc.).
- **Kalgoorlie cracking/leaching plant** (Western Australia): designed to move the first processing stage out of Malaysia and closer to mine, reducing Malaysia-related regulatory/waste risk.
- **US Heavy Rare Earth facility in Texas** (Seadrift): strategic build-out targeting HREE separation in the U.S. with DoD support — a direct answer to the Dy/Tb chokepoint.

### Single points of failure
- **Regulatory/environmental**: Malaysia plant has repeatedly faced waste-disposal and licensing opposition; any prolonged shutdown materially constrains Western oxide supply.
- **Tailings/waste handling**: ion-adjacent processing produces radioactive-bearing residues (thorium/uranium associated with monazite), making waste management a political and engineering bottleneck.
- **Settlement of trade**: Lynas is an Australian company with Malaysia processing and U.S. expansion — three jurisdictions, each adding export/import, environmental, and geopolitical risk.

### Strategic position
- Lynas is the benchmark for "non-Chinese separated oxide" and thus the entity most directly comparable to Chinese processors on cost and reliability.
- Its HREE build-out (Texas) is the critical test of whether Western separation of Dy/Tb can be revived at commercial scale.

## The Diversification Stack Around Them

- **Policy scaffolding**: EU Critical Raw Materials Act (2030 benchmarks: 10% domestic extraction, 40% domestic processing, 25% recycling), DPA Title III in the U.S., DoD/OSC capital, and the December 2025 EU export restrictions on magnet waste/battery scrap.
- **Supply-side complements**: ARPA-E REACT (magnet alternatives), DOE ~$72M for domestic critical minerals and ultra-powerful magnets, and recycling economics to extend the effective supply beyond primary mine output.
- **Substitution pressure**: ferrite magnets and rare-earth-free motor designs compete with NdFeB; heavy-rare-earth substitution (grain-boundary diffusion, etc.) reduces but does not eliminate Dy/Tb demand.
- **Price/volume signal**: Chinese price responses and export-control pause (ending November 10, 2026) create a known pressure point for Western projects to hit milestones before the next tightening.

## Risk Matrix for the Two-Column Strategy

| Risk | MP Materials | Lynas | Mitigation lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation tech gap | HREE separation still being built (OSC loan) | Established for LREE; HREE in Texas | DoD/OSC capital, HREE facility target |
| Magnet-making | Early-stage (Fort Worth) | Limited/indirect today | DoD offtake, DPA Title III |
| Regulatory/environmental | Permitting and waste in U.S. | Malaysia waste disputes | Kalgoorlie, U.S. site diversification |
| Cost vs Chinese processors | Higher unit costs at scale-up | Higher logistics/regulatory costs | Price floors, policy subsidies |
| Geopolitical leverage | China export-control pause (Nov 2026) | China price response/trade measures | Stockpiles, new suppliers, substitution |
| Financial dependency | High (DoD equity) | Moderate | offtake contracts, strategic capital |
