# Influence Operations Doctrine & Offensive Techniques

**Status:** STABLE

## Overview

Influence operations (IO) are coordinated activities designed to shape the perceptions, attitudes, and behaviors of target audiences to achieve political, military, or economic objectives. This page covers the doctrinal foundations, offensive techniques, and strategic frameworks used by both state and non-state actors. It complements the [[influence-operations-detection-countermeasures|detection-focused]] sibling page by examining how influence campaigns are designed, deployed, and evolved — the attacker's perspective necessary for effective defense.

---

## 1. PSYOP Doctrine and the Intelligence Cycle

### 1.1 Historical Evolution

Psychological Operations (PSYOP) — recently restored as the official term over the euphemistic "Military Information Support Operations" (MISO) by a 2025 Department of War directive — have evolved from leaflets and loudspeakers to AI-augmented, multi-domain cognitive warfare. The foundational principle remains constant: effectiveness depends on deep audience understanding.

Tihan (2026) articulates the constitutive relationship between the strategic intelligence cycle and PSYOP efficacy. Contemporary PSYOP is not an isolated communication activity but an operational dimension of national power whose value as a non-kinetic force multiplier is intrinsically dependent on intelligence depth.

### 1.2 Intelligence-Driven PSYOP: The SOCINT Foundation

Two canonical case studies demonstrate the pattern:

- **Gulf War (1991):** Intelligence requirements focused on socio-cultural profiling (SOCINT) of Iraqi forces generated tailored, credible persuasive messages. The result: strategic demoralization and mass surrender — a tangible operational outcome from non-kinetic means.
- **Operation Just Cause (Panama, 1989):** In a complex urban environment, intelligence-driven PSYOP enabled efficient neutralization of adversaries while protecting civilians.

The organic integration of the intelligence cycle into PSYOP planning and execution is a strategic imperative for gaining cognitive advantage.

### 1.3 US Army PSYOP Modernization

The Psychological Operations Qualification Course has transitioned from a Global War on Terrorism focus (large-population messaging, counter-radicalization) to Large-Scale Combat Operations against peer adversaries (SWCS, 2025–2026). The curriculum now emphasizes influencing how adversary commanders make decisions, not just populations.

## 2. Cognitive Warfare Frameworks

### 2.1 NATO's Cognitive Warfare Concept

NATO Allied Command Transformation defines cognitive warfare as the synchronization of adversarial effects against emotional and subconscious domains, exploiting the human mind as the battlespace (NATO ACT, 2025). It operates below the threshold of armed conflict, targeting cognition itself — perception, reasoning, decision-making — rather than information channels alone.

### 2.2 Academic Formalization

Rushing, Hersch & Xu (arXiv:2603.05222, 2026) propose a unified definition and interaction framework grounded in the OODA loop (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act), with measurable attributes for cognitive superiority. Their key contribution: treating cognitive operations as attacker-defender interactions rather than a subset of information operations, enabling systematic evaluation of when cognitive advantage has been achieved.

Van Diggelen et al. (arXiv:2504.11486, 2025) address the ethical dimension of AI-enabled countermeasures: maintaining meaningful human control in automated defense against cognitive warfare on social media platforms, parallel to the Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems (LAWS) debate.

## 3. State Actor Approaches

### 3.1 Russian Active Measures (aktivnyye meropriyatiya)

Modern Russian information warfare is a direct continuation of Soviet-era "active measures" — covert strategies using disinformation, front organizations, and media control (CEPA, 2025). Key characteristics:

- **Doctrinal continuity:** From Soviet KGB through modern GRU/SVR/FSO, active measures are treated as a permanent feature of strategy, not episodic campaigns.
- **Platform exploitation:** Russia uses all narrative-transmission platforms — media, conferences, international frameworks, diplomatic channels (ISW, 2025).
- **Hybrid warfare integration:** Information operations are synchronized with cyber attacks, economic coercion, and covert action below the war threshold.
- **AI-enabled amplification:** AI-driven propaganda and deepfake technologies have escalated both scale and sophistication.

### 3.2 Chinese Three Warfares (san zhong zhanfa)

China operationalizes information warfare through the "Three Warfares" doctrine:

1. **Media Warfare (yulun zhan):** Shaping public opinion through state-controlled media narratives.
2. **Psychological Warfare (xinli zhan):** Influencing adversary decision-makers and populations.
3. **Legal Warfare (falü zhan):** Using international law and norms as instruments of strategic competition.

Bliss (AFGC thesis, 2025) identifies that both China and Russia have industrialized information warfare as a central pillar of national strategy, with China focusing on long-term narrative dominance and Russia on operational disruption.

### 3.3 Sino-Russian Convergence

CEPA (Matura, 2025) documents growing alignment between Russian and Chinese FIMI (Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference) operations. While formal coordination remains limited, both employ:
- State-controlled media amplification of each other's narratives
- AI-generated content for scale
- Exploitation of Western media freedom to create polarization
- Local influencer networks for credibility laundering
- Shared anti-Western narratives promoting multipolarity

The Hybrid CoE paper (March 2026) further documents AI-driven FIMI from both China and Russia as a synchronized threat.

## 4. Offensive Techniques Taxonomy

### 4.1 Psychological Operations (PSYOP)
- Tailored persuasive messaging based on SOCINT profiles
- Demographic and psychographic microtargeting
- Narrative engineering to exploit cognitive biases
- Credibility laundering through front organizations

### 4.2 Military Deception (MILDEC)
- See [[deception-operations-intelligence-history]] for detailed historical doctrine: US JP 3-13.4 (See-Think-Do), Soviet maskirovka, Chinese stratagems
- Magruder's Principle: deceptions succeed by confirming pre-existing beliefs, not by changing them
- Modern applications: deepfake-based tactical deception, false flag cyber operations

### 4.3 Social Media Manipulation
- Bot networks for artificial amplification (see [[social-media-profile-analysis-osint]] for detection methodology)
- Inauthentic coordinated behavior: state-sponsored troll farms, influence-for-hire services
- Engagement optimization exploitation: gaming platform recommendation algorithms
- Microtargeting of vulnerable populations with polarizing content

### 4.4 AI-Augmented Techniques
- Generative AI for synthetic media: deepfakes, synthetic audio, AI-generated text indistinguishable from human writing (see [[influence-operations-detection-countermeasures]] for text-to-behavioral detection shift)
- Automated narrative personalization at scale
- LLM-driven conversational influence agents
- AI-powered content localization for cross-cultural campaigns

### 4.5 Cyber-Enabled Psychological Operations
- Doxing and strategic leaks
- Weaponized transparency: selective document releases
- Compromised credential exploitation for account takeover and impersonation
- Integration of hack-and-leak with narrative amplification

### 4.6 Hybrid Campaign Architecture

CEPA (2025) describes the hybrid threat imperative: Russia's strategy exploits the seams of open societies — disinformation, cyber, sabotage, coercion — synchronized below the war threshold. The hybrid campaign is not a menu of isolated tactics but an orchestrated system where each component reinforces the others. Defensive fragmentation (counter-disinformation separate from cyber defense separate from economic resilience) is the primary vulnerability.

## 5. Case Studies

### 5.1 Gulf War (1991) — PSYOP as Force Multiplier
Intelligence-driven leaflet campaigns and radio broadcasts targeting Iraqi soldiers' specific fears and grievances led to 87,000 surrenders. The campaign demonstrated that tailored messaging grounded in SOCINT could achieve effects equivalent to kinetic operations.

### 5.2 Russian Election Interference (2016–2024)
The Internet Research Agency (IRA) campaign pioneered industrial-scale social media manipulation: fake personas, targeted ads, organized events, and divisive content. The 2016 operation informed a doctrinal evolution: from episodic election interference to sustained narrative warfare.

### 5.3 Chinese Vaccine Diplomacy Narratives (2020–2022)
During COVID-19, China deployed coordinated media narratives promoting its vaccines while amplifying Western vaccine skepticism — a two-pronged strategy undermining trust in Western institutions while building Chinese soft power.

### 5.4 Operation Sindoor (2026) — Information Warfare in Limited Conflict
India's military response to the Pahalgam attack demonstrated a doctrinal shift integrating information warfare with kinetic operations under the nuclear threshold. The calibrated use of air power combined with narrative control and OSINT exploitation, illustrating limited-war-information-warfare integration.

## 7. Key Structural Insight

Influence operations doctrine and entity resolution are structurally isomorphic: both are confidence-weighted, multi-source corroboration problems. In entity resolution, we match records across heterogeneous datasets using probabilistic linkage. In IO attribution, we match campaign artifacts (narratives, accounts, infrastructure) across platforms using behavioral and linguistic signatures. The Fellegi-Sunter framework — modeling match probability (m) and unmatch probability (u) — maps directly to IO attribution confidence scoring. This isomorphism suggests that entity resolution methodologies developed for OSINT ([[entity-resolution-frameworks]]) have direct applicability to influence operations analysis.
