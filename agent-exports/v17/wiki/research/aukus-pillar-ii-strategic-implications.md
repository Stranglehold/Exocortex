## Overview

AUKUS Pillar II (Advanced Capabilities) is the technology-sharing and capability-development component of the trilateral Australia-United Kingdom-United States security partnership, distinct from Pillar I's nuclear-powered submarine program (SSN-AUKUS). Announced alongside Pillar I in September 2021, Pillar II aims to "foster deeper integration of security and defence-related science, technology, industrial bases, and supply chains" across all three nations.

While Pillar I represents a one-time submarine acquisition, Pillar II is the enduring engine of trilateral technological integration — and arguably the more strategically significant component in the long run.

## Technology Domains

The CRS report (R47599, v3, May 2024) identifies at least eight active working groups, six addressing technological areas and two functional:

### 1. Hypersonics & Counter-Hypersonics
- Hypersonic Flight Test and Experimentation (HyFliTE) Project Arrangement signed November 2024 enabling shared testing facilities, technical data, and accelerated development of hypersonic vehicles and counter-hypersonic systems
- Allows the three nations to use each other's hypersonic test infrastructure, compressing development timelines
- Includes both offensive hypersonic weapons (boost-glide vehicles, scramjet cruise missiles) and defensive counter-hypersonic capabilities

### 2. Artificial Intelligence & Autonomy
- AI for military decision-making, autonomous systems integration, and battlespace awareness
- Cross-domain AI applications across all other Pillar II technology areas
- Key challenge: aligning ethical frameworks and rules of engagement across three nations with different legal traditions regarding autonomous weapons

### 3. Quantum Technologies
- Positioning, Navigation, and Timing (PNT) — quantum sensors as GPS-denied alternative
- Quantum computing for defense applications (cryptography, materials science, optimization)
- Quantum communications and secure networking
- Australia's quantum research base (Silicon Quantum Computing, UNSW) as a strategic differentiator

### 4. Undersea Capabilities
- June 2, 2026: First dedicated Pillar II project announced — advanced undersea military capabilities (Australian Defence)
- May 30, 2026: Pentagon chief confirms unmanned undersea vehicles with delivery target 2027 (Reuters)
- Autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) for seabed warfare, mine countermeasures, intelligence collection
- Undersea sensor networks for maritime domain awareness in the Indo-Pacific

### 5. Advanced Cyber Capabilities
- Offensive and defensive cyber operations integration
- Trilateral engagement on cyber security with critical suppliers to strengthen cyber capabilities (BAE Systems, March 2026)
- Cyber workforce development and information sharing
- Protecting Pillar I submarine technology from cyber espionage

### 6. Electronic Warfare
- Electronic attack, electronic protection, and electronic support
- Integration with cyber capabilities for full-spectrum electromagnetic operations
- Growing importance given Ukraine conflict lessons on EW in contested environments

### 7. Information Sharing
- Enhanced intelligence sharing infrastructure
- Secure communications between AUKUS partners
- Cross-domain data interoperability standards

### 8. Critical Minerals (Proposed)
- ASPI's John Coyne (June 2023) proposed critical minerals as an additional Pillar II area
- Relevance: rare earth processing, battery supply chains, defense industrial resilience

## Governance & Organization

- Working groups: six technology-specific + two functional (CRS, 2024)
- ITAR exemption struggle: As of April 2024, both House Foreign Affairs Chair McCaul and Senate Foreign Relations Ranking Member Risch criticized the administration's failure to certify UK and Australia for ITAR exemptions — a persistent barrier to seamless technology sharing
- Defense Trade Cooperation Treaties: U.S. has separate treaties with UK (2007) and Australia (2007), but AUKUS-specific exemptions remain incomplete
- Pillar II operates under a non-binding instrument framework, allowing flexibility but creating uncertainty about long-term commitment

## Strategic Implications

### 1. Indo-Pacific Deterrence Architecture
Pillar II fills the technology gap between U.S. capabilities and allied forces, creating a more credible conventional deterrence posture against China. The undersea domain is particularly critical given China's naval expansion and the vulnerability of undersea cables and infrastructure.

### 2. Industrial Base Integration
Unlike traditional Foreign Military Sales, Pillar II aims for genuine industrial base integration — shared R&D, co-production, and supply chain interdependence. This creates strategic entanglement that raises the cost of abandoning the partnership.

### 3. Technology Sharing vs. Security Barriers
ITAR and related export control regimes remain the primary obstacle. The 2024 failure to certify UK and Australia for exemptions indicates domestic political constraints — Congressional concerns about technology leakage compete with strategic imperatives for sharing.

### 4. China's Response Calculus
AUKUS Pillar II directly challenges China's military-technological modernization. The response is likely: accelerated indigenous development, increased espionage targeting AUKUS technology, and diplomatic pressure on regional states to reject AUKUS cooperation.

### 5. NATO/European Dimension
AUKUS excludes EU/NATO members beyond the UK, creating tension with France (post-submarine deal cancellation) and raising questions about whether AUKUS complements or competes with NATO technological initiatives like DIANA.
