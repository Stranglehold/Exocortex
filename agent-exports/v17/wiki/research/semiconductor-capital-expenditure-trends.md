## Overview

Semiconductor manufacturing is one of the most capital-intensive industries globally. Leading-edge chip fabrication plants (fabs) cost $20-40 billion each. Capital expenditure (capex) trends serve as a leading indicator for technological competition, supply chain security, geopolitical strategy, and AI infrastructure buildout. Global semiconductor capex is projected to reach **$200 billion in 2026**, a 20% year-over-year increase from $166 billion in 2025 (Semiconductor Intelligence, April 2026).

## Major Foundry Capex Commitments

### TSMC
- **2026 capex: $52-56B** (Q4 2025 guidance), up from ~$37B in 2025
- N3 (3nm) production ramping at Fab 18 (Tainan); N2 (2nm) pilot in Hsinchu and Kaohsiung
- A14 (1.4nm) development underway; target 2028-2029 production
- Arizona Fab 1 (4nm) in volume production; Fab 2 (3nm) targeting Q3 2026; Fab 3 (2nm or A14) announced
- Total Arizona investment: $65B across three fabs
- Japan Kumamoto Fab 1 (28/22nm) operational; Fab 2 (6/7nm) under construction
- Germany Dresden fab (28/22nm, automotive) ground broken August 2024, targeting 2027 production

### Samsung Electronics
- **2026 capex: ~$73.24B** (110 trillion KRW), the single largest annual semiconductor investment ever (announced March 19, 2026)
- Pyeongtaek campus expansion: memory (DRAM, NAND) and foundry logic
- Taylor, Texas fab: $17B initial investment, 4nm and below, targeting 2026 production
- GAA (Gate-All-Around) 3nm process in production; 2nm GAA targeting 2027
- Memory capex driven by HBM3E/HBM4 demand for AI accelerators (NVIDIA B200, subsequent generations)

### Intel
- **2025 capex: $17.7B**; 2026 flat-to-down as restructuring continues
- Ohio "Silicon Heartland": $28B for two fabs, construction delayed to 2027-2028 production
- Arizona Fab 52/62: 18A (1.8nm) process, targeting 2025-2026
- Germany Magdeburg fab: $33B project delayed/paused due to EU subsidy negotiations and financial constraints
- Intel Foundry Services (IFS) strategy pivoting: 18A as internal product node first, external foundry customers later
- CHIPS Act award: $8.5B direct funding + $11B loans; disbursement tied to construction milestones

### SMIC (China)
- Capex heavily skewed toward mature nodes (28nm and above) due to US export controls on advanced equipment (EUV, advanced DUV)
- SMIC 5nm N+3 with DUV multi-patterning: yields 30-40%, targeting 2026-2027 3nm GAA
- China Big Fund III: $47.5B launched 2024 for domestic semiconductor equipment and materials
- Domestic equipment maturation: Naura (etch/deposition), AMEC (etch), SMEE (lithography) progressing but years behind ASML/AMAT
- Total China semiconductor self-sufficiency rate: ~23% in 2025, government targeting 70% by 2030 (CSIS, 2025)

### Memory Makers
- SK Hynix: $15-18B 2026 capex, HBM3E leadership (sole NVIDIA B200 HBM supplier), HBM4 development
- Micron: $14-16B 2026, NY and Idaho fabs under construction, HBM3E qualification, CHIPS Act $6.1B award
- Kioxia/Western Digital: capex recovering from 2023 trough, BiCS FLASH 218-layer NAND

## Equipment Manufacturer Trends

### ASML
- EUV monopoly: 0.33 NA EUV (NXE:3800E, $200M+ per tool); High-NA EUV (EXE:5200, $380M+ per tool)
- 2025 net bookings: ~€12B in Q4 2025 alone; backlog ~€40B
- 2026 shipment target: ~50-60 EUV systems, 5-10 High-NA
- Intel: first High-NA EUV installation (Oregon); TSMC: High-NA adoption for A14 node (2028+); Samsung: High-NA for 2nm

### Applied Materials, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron, KLA
- Wafer fab equipment (WFE) market: ~$110-120B in 2025, projected $120-130B in 2026
- GAA transistor transition (3nm→2nm) driving etch/deposition intensity: ~30% more steps per wafer
- Backside power delivery (Intel PowerVia, TSMC Super PowerRail) adding new process steps
- China pulling in equipment purchases ahead of potential further export controls (2025-2026)

## Equipment Export Controls

- October 2022: US BIS export controls on advanced logic (≤14nm), DRAM (≤18nm), NAND (≥128 layers)
- January 2023: Netherlands (ASML) joins — restricts NXT:2000i and above DUV, all EUV
- January 2023: Japan (Tokyo Electron, etc.) joins — restricts immersion tools
- October 2023: BIS updates to close loopholes (performance density thresholds)
- 2025-2026: Controls tightening — discussion of restricting equipment maintenance/services in China, expanding to mature nodes
- MATCH Act (2026 proposal): entity-based controls, would block TSMC from servicing Chinese clients without license

### CHIPS Act Disbursement Status (as of 2026)
- Total awards announced: ~$36B across 20+ companies
- Actual disbursed: significantly less — tied to construction, production, and financial milestones
- Key recipients: Intel ($8.5B), TSMC ($6.6B), Samsung ($6.4B), Micron ($6.1B), GlobalFoundries ($1.5B)

- GAO identified challenges: workforce shortages (50,000+ technician gap), environmental review delays, construction cost inflation

## Capex-to-Revenue Ratio: Overinvestment Signal

Semiconductor capex-to-revenue ratio historically averages 15-18%. Ratios above 20% signal potential overinvestment and impending capacity glut:
- TSMC 2026E: ~19-20% ($52-56B / ~$270-290B revenue)
- Samsung 2026E: ~29% ($73.24B / ~$250B semiconductor revenue) — historically elevated
- Intel 2025: ~33% ($17.7B / ~$53B) — high but from restructuring, not expansion
- Memory industry 2024-2025: ~25-30% during recovery from 2023 trough; 2026 normalization expected

## AI-Driven Demand Structural Shift

- AI chip (GPU/ASIC) TAM: $100B+ in 2026, up from ~$45B in 2024
- NVIDIA B200 (Blackwell) and successor architectures consume entire TSMC 3nm and CoWoS-L capacity allocations
- CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) advanced packaging: TSMC expanding from 15,000 wpm (2023) to 60,000+ wpm (2026)
- HBM memory: 2026 demand ~2.5x 2025 levels; SK Hynix >50% market share
- Power constraint: AI data centers consuming 50-100MW each; electricity availability becoming site selection factor

## Capex Cycle Dynamics and Forecasting

Semiconductor capex follows a 3-4 year cycle historically:
1. Undersupply → price increases → margin expansion
2. Margin expansion → aggressive capex → capacity overshoot
3. Overshoot → price declines → margin compression
4. Margin compression → capex cuts → return to undersupply

Current cycle (2023-2026): AI-driven demand has broken the traditional cycle pattern. Unlike prior cycles (PC, smartphone), AI training/inference demand has no visible saturation point, leading to sustained elevated capex across logic, memory, and packaging.

Key leading indicators to monitor:
- ASML quarterly bookings and backlog (6-9 month lead on equipment demand)
- TSMC capacity utilization rates (quarterly)
- DRAM/NAND spot prices (Biweekly: DRAMeXchange, TrendForce)
- Foundry wafer price trends (TSMC 3nm wafer: ~$20,000 in 2025; 2nm expected $25,000+)
- Hyperscaler (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) capex guidance — combined $240B+ in 2026

## Cross-Domain Connections

1. **Defense Procurement Cycles:** Leading-edge chips are critical for defense systems (Aegis, F-35, satellite payloads). CHIPS Act reserves $2B for defense applications. TSMC Arizona directly addresses "trusted foundry" requirements for DoD.

2. **US-China Semiconductor Supply Chain:** Export controls are the primary weapon in technology competition. ASML's EUV monopoly and applied materials' deposition tools are the chokepoints. Capex trends reveal sanctions adaptation.

3. **Bridging Local-to-Frontier Model Performance:** Hardware availability (H100/B200 vs consumer GPUs) directly constrains local model inference. Capex trends determine GPU availability and pricing for consumer GPUs.

4. **Electric Utility & Critical Infrastructure:** Fabs consume massive electricity (TSMC alone ~5% of Taiwan's power). Grid capacity is becoming a fab siting constraint (Arizona water, Ohio power availability).

5. **Energy Commodity Dynamics:** Semiconductor manufacturing is energy-intensive. Taiwan's electricity prices, natural gas availability for fabs in US Southwest, and renewable energy mandates intersect with capex decisions.

6. **Agentic AI Self-Learning:** AI chip demand (training + inference) is the primary driver of 2024-2026 capex cycle. Inference chip specialization (Groq LPU, Cerebras, d-Matrix) may alter capex allocation patterns.

7. **Multi-Agent Orchestration Patterns:** Distributed AI inference across GPU clusters requires high-bandwidth interconnects (NVLink, InfiniBand). Capex in networking equipment and optics parallels GPU capex.

8. **Supply Chain Economic Warfare:** Semiconductor capex is a national security metric. China's Big Fund investments are explicitly aimed at achieving self-sufficiency; US/EU/Japan/Korea counter with subsidies. Capex reveals strategic intent.

9. **Rare Earth Supply Chains:** Specialty materials (high-purity silicon, neon, C4F6, photoresist) are capex enablers/constraints. Neon supply disruption risk (Ukraine war, 50% of global semiconductor-grade neon pre-war).

10. **Intelligence Failure Analysis:** Past capex cycles (2000 dot-com, 2008 financial crisis, 2019 memory downturn) demonstrate systematic industry overestimation of demand persistence. Structured analytic techniques (reference class forecasting) can mitigate cognitive biases in capex forecasting.
