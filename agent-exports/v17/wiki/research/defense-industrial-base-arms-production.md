# Defense Industrial Base & Arms Production Economics

**Status:** STABLE  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-03  
**Lines:** ~85  

## Summary

Analysis of the post-2022 conventional munitions production surge as an economic system: peacetime-to-wartime rate transitions, capacity bottlenecks across the full ammunition chain (shell bodies, energetics, propellants, fuzes), learning-curve and lead-time dynamics, and the strategic role of visible industrial capacity as economic deterrence. Grounded corpus-first in Exocortex defense pages with a 2026 web gap-fill on 155mm production programs.
## Key Facts

- US 155mm ramp: ~14K rounds/month (2022) to 100K/month target; ~$4.5B invested. Actual ~40K/month late 2025 (40% of target).
- US 2026 goal: over 1M 155mm shells; plus 30K cluster shells/year plan (Nov 2025).
- Rheinmetall: 1.5M 155mm shells/year by 2027, exceeding combined US output.
- EU: ~650K large-caliber munitions/year via EDIP/SAFE; declared vs usable throughput gap persists.
## Economics of the Surge

- Peacetime ammunition markets are batch/order-driven with small order books; wartime rates require capital-intensive new lines, typically 12-24 months, plus workforce, tooling, and test/QA capacity.
- Produced shell count is gated by the scarcest downstream step (energetic filling, propellant, fuzes), not shell-body capacity alone - a hard constraint ignored by headline capacity announcements.
- Unit costs fall with volume along the learning curve, but fixed costs are high; surge economics favor multi-year contracts (SAFE, EDIP, OTA/MTA) that justify plant investment ahead of demand.
- Defense procurement is de facto industrial policy: ~$1T DoD budget plus FY2026 supplemental makes procurement timing, vendor selection, and program priority into industrial policy levers.
- Economic deterrence (NDIS priority 4) is the strategic rationale for advertising capacity: visible industrial throughput raises adversary cost-imposition estimates.
## Industrial Base Structure (2026)

- Primes: Rheinmetall, General Dynamics, BAE Systems, Northrop Grumman, RTX, Lockheed Martin, Leonardo, KNDS, Kongsberg, Thales.
- Munitions specialists: Nammo, Chemring, Elbit, Hanwha, GD-OTS, Nexter/KNDS, IMT (Slovenia), Mesquite/GD-AMS.
- Shipbuilding: Columbia-class #1 acquisition priority; $16.65B monthly awards across Columbia/Virginia; disruption cascades across fleet.
- Platform-based acquisition shift: Anduril Lattice $20B consolidates 120+ procurement pathways into one IDIQ vehicle.
- Allied co-production: five new NDIS-IMP agreements (Jun 2026) cut single-point-of-failure risk in shells, precision missiles, drone subsystems.
## Bottlenecks & Failure Modes (empirically observed)

- Shell-body forging, propellant (nitrocellulose, cotton-pulp feedstock), energetic filling (safety/permit-limited lines), fuzes, skilled labor, tooling.
- IG findings: Mesquite plant and shell-body bottlenecks; output below plan despite $4.5B invested.
- European gap: declared objectives vs effective industrial throughput; common procurement aggregates demand but does not create capacity by itself.
- US: 40K/month actual vs 100K target; Breaking Defense (Jul 2026) spotlighted the Texas facility.
