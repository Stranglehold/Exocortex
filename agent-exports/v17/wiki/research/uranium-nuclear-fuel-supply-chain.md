## Overview

The nuclear fuel supply chain is a compact, highly concentrated energy commodity chain with the same single-point-of-dependency structure the Exocortex corpus already tracks for rare earths, SPR, and Russian oil. It runs through six stages: mining (U3O8), conversion (UF6), enrichment (LEU / U-235; HALEU for advanced reactors), fuel fabrication, reactor operation, and spent-fuel storage/reprocessing. Concentration is highest in the middle two stages: conversion and enrichment, where Russia (Rosatom/Tenex) and Kazakhstan (Kazatomprom) dominate, creating a geopolitical choke similar to China's rare-earth separation dominance.

This page maps the chain, its 2026 policy/investment inflection (US uranium import ban waivers, HALEU/SMR demand pull, long-term contracting reset), and its OSINT monitoring surface.

## The Fuel Cycle at a Glance

| Stage | Function | Key participants | Concentration risk |
|-------|----------|------------------|--------------------|
| Mining | U3O8 concentrate production | Kazakhstan (Kazatomprom), Canada (Cameco), Australia, Namibia, Niger, Uzbekistan | Kazakhstan ~40% of world mine supply; top 5 ~80% |

| Reactor | Power generation (including SMR/advanced reactors) | Global fleet ~440 reactors | — |
| Back end | Used fuel storage, reprocessing | Orano, Rosatom (Mayak), Rokkasho | — |

## Chokepoint Structure: The Russian Middle

- **Conversion/enrichment concentration**: Russian state-controlled Rosatom and its umbrella Tenex historically supplied roughly 40% of world conversion and 40-45% of enrichment services, with European and US utilities structurally dependent on Russian LEU, especially for VVER reactors but also through contracted SWU into Western fuel supply.
- **The 'Russia exception' pattern**: Westinghouse/Framatome fuel for US and Western European pressurized water reactors still relies in part on Russian-enriched product and Russian-sourced or converted material — the same 'structural dependency in the middle stage' seen in rare-earth processing.
- **Kazakhstan coupling**: Kazatomprom supplies ~40% of world U3O8 and routes conversion/enrichment partly through Russia (and increasingly through China/Western Path), making Central Asian export logistics a chokepoint parallel to OPEC tanker routes.

- **US Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act (2024)**: phased ban with waiver window; executive-issued waivers have kept some Russian LEU flowing into 2026 while domestic capacity scales. Verify current waiver posture.
- **HALEU demand pull**: high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU, 10-20% U-235) is required by most advanced/SMR designs; US HALEU procurement (Centrus cascade, HALEU enrichment demonstration, DOE contracting) is the binding constraint on the SMR/datacenter build-out.
- **Strategic reserves**: US LEU reserve / HALEU reserve plans mirror SPR logic — build a buffer while dependency persists; DOE and commercial contracting (~$2-3B+ multi-year uranium/SWU contracts, 2024-2026) reset the long-term contracting cycle after a decade of underinvestment.
- **SMR/AI datacenter demand arc**: nuclear re-emerged as firm clean power for datacenters; reactor orders, fuel contracting, and enrichment capacity are the pacing items; term uranium/SWU prices and investment announcements are the leading indicators.

## Web-Verified Facts (2026-08-07)

- **US import ban mechanics**: the Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act (H.R. 1042) was signed May 13, 2024 and took effect August 12, 2024. It prohibits Russian-origin uranium product imports through 2040, but permits DOE (with State/Commerce consultation) to issue waivers through January 1, 2028 where no alternative supply exists or purchase is in the national interest (Covington & Burling 2024; US State Dept.; World Nuclear News — Centrus received a July 2024 DOE waiver for Russian LEU deliveries in 2024-2025).
- **Persistent dependency**: despite the ban, Russian uranium still powers roughly a fifth of US electricity through the waiver loophole; the US market accounts for about half of Rosatom's enriched-uranium exports while Russian-origin material covers ~25% of US enrichment needs (Moscow Times 2025-03-13; Yahoo News 2026). Rosatom/Tenex holds ~40% of global enrichment capacity and ~17% of fuel fabrication (The National Interest 2026).
- **Conversion oligopoly**: world uranium conversion runs through exactly five commercial plants — Cameco (Canada), CNNC (China), Orano (France), Rosatom (Russia), ConverDyn (US restart) — making conversion the tightest single stage (Raw Science 2026).
- **HALEU demand pull**: Centrus delivered the first 900 kg HALEU to DOE (June 2025) from Piketon, Ohio — the only US facility licensed to enrich up to 19.75% U-235 and the first US-owned enrichment production since the 1950s (DOE, NucNet). On January 5, 2026 DOE finalized $2.7B task orders over ten years — American Centrifuge Operating ($900M), General Matter ($900M), Centrus ($900M) — to expand domestic LEU/HALEU enrichment capacity (DOE Office of Nuclear Energy).
- **SMR/datacenter nexus**: HALEU is the binding constraint for most advanced/SMR designs; DOE and commercial multi-year uranium/SWU contract resets are the leading indicators of the long-cycle rebuild; firm clean power for datacenters re-emerged as the demand driver for new reactor orders (2026 EIA/industry signals).
