## Overview

Financial Intelligence (FININT) applies alternative data sources not for investment alpha but for detecting financial crime: money laundering, sanctions evasion, terrorist financing, fraud, and corruption. While the investment industry uses alternative data to predict earnings, FININT uses it to surface hidden networks, identify beneficial owners, trace illicit fund flows, and reconstruct financial relationships that bad actors deliberately obscure.

The core intelligence question parallels OSINT entity resolution: **how do you take heterogeneous, unstructured, or deliberately obfuscated datasets and resolve entities across them to expose non-obvious connections?** The difference: FININT operates against adversarial actors who actively conceal their tracks.

**Market context:** The alternative data market reached $12B in 2025 with a projected $168.2B by 2034 (IMARC Group 2025). 78% of hedge funds had integrated alternative data by 2022 — but FININT adoption by financial crime units lags significantly, creating an asymmetry that sophisticated criminals exploit.

**Library grounding:** Hands-On Machine Learning for Algorithmic Trading (Jansen 2020, Chapter 3) provides the canonical framework for evaluating alternative datasets — the same criteria apply to FININT with adversarial hardening. The book identifies three major source categories (individuals, business processes, sensors/IoT) and five evaluation dimensions (time horizon, frequency, reliability, latency, format), detailed below.

**2026 regulatory context:** The US Corporate Transparency Act (CTA) beneficial ownership reporting requirement was effectively suspended for US companies and persons by FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule, removing what had been anticipated as a ~35M-filing BOI Registry. Foreign reporting companies still face deadlines, creating an asymmetric intelligence gap: US shell companies remain opaque while foreign entities face increasing disclosure. This is the single most significant FININT data gap as of 2026.

## 1. Alternative Data Source Taxonomy (from Library)

Jansen (2020) classifies alternative data into three source categories, each with distinct FININT applications:

### 1.1 Sources Generated by Individuals
Social media posts, e-commerce activity, search engine activity, mobile app usage, messaging traffic. For FININT:
- Social media can reveal lifestyle indicators inconsistent with declared income (corruption detection)
- E-commerce activity can identify shell company operations (Amazon storefronts linked to sanctioned entities)
- Messaging traffic metadata reveals communication networks among co-conspirators
- Social graph analysis identifies hidden relationships between ostensibly unrelated entities

### 1.2 Sources Generated by Business Processes
Payment card transaction data, company exhaust data (banking records, scanner data, supply chain orders), trade flow data, corporate payment records. For FININT:
- **Credit card / payment data**: Transaction pattern analysis for structuring detection (<$10K deposits to evade CTRs), funnel account identification, and money mule network mapping
- **Company exhaust**: Supply chain order data for trade-based money laundering (TBML) detection — over/under-invoicing, phantom shipments, duplicate invoicing
- **Trade flow data**: Customs declaration cross-validation with shipping manifests and financial flows
- **Market microstructure data**: Order book analysis for market manipulation detection (wash trading, spoofing)

### 1.3 Sources Generated by Sensors/IoT
Satellite imagery, AIS vessel tracking, geolocation data, IoT sensor feeds. For FININT:
- **AIS data**: Vessel dark-activity detection for sanctions evasion (Iranian shadow fleet, Russian oil price cap circumvention)
- **Satellite imagery**: Port throughput monitoring vs. declared trade volumes, illicit mining detection
- **Geolocation data**: Sanctioned individual travel pattern correlation with financial transaction locations

### 1.4 Five Evaluation Dimensions (Adversarial Extension)

Jansen's five criteria, extended for adversarial FININT contexts:

| Dimension | Investment Application | FININT Adversarial Extension |
|-----------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| **Time horizon** | Backtesting length requirement | Adversaries change evasion patterns faster than backtesting windows; requires online learning |
| **Frequency** | Signal differentiation | Lower frequency creates detection gaps that structured criminals exploit (quarterly reporting vs real-time laundering) |
| **Reliability** | Data accuracy audit | Adversaries inject disinformation into alternative data sources (false vessel registrations, fabricated trade documents) |
| **Latency** | Trading execution speed | Each day of latency is a day criminals have to move funds; BSA filing 30-60 day lag creates structural detection gap |
| **Format** | Ease of processing | Data format heterogeneity across jurisdictions is a deliberate obfuscation technique by sophisticated launderers |

## 2. Primary FININT Data Sources (2026 Landscape)

### 2.1 Regulatory Filings

| Source | Jurisdiction | Volume | Key ER Fields | 2026 Status |
|--------|-------------|--------|---------------|-------------|
| **FinCEN SARs** | US | 4M+/year | Names, addresses, phones, account numbers, narrative text | Active; 87% of IRS-CI investigations involve BSA filing on primary subject (Quantexa 2025) |
| **FinCEN CTRs** | US | 21M+/year | Names, addresses, SSN/EIN, account numbers, transaction amounts | Active; structuring detection via <$10K aggregation |
| **FinCEN FBARs** | US | ~1.5M/year | Foreign account numbers, institution names | Active; critical for offshore account detection |
| **BOI Registry** | US | ~0 (suspended) | Beneficial owners, company applicants | Suspended for US companies/persons (FinCEN interim final rule, March 2025); foreign entities still required |
| **EU Beneficial Ownership Registers** | EU | Multi-million | UBO data, entity identifiers | Active but with CJEU privacy ruling limitations; varies by member state |
| **UK Companies House** | UK | 5M+ companies | PSC register, officers, filings | Active; publicly searchable; PSC register mandatory since 2016 |

The suspension of US BOI reporting is the most significant FININT data gap in 2026. With no mandatory beneficial ownership disclosure for US entities, shell company detection relies entirely on third-party data and investigative inference — [[corporate-registry-investigation-osint]] provides alternative methods.

### 2.2 International Payment Networks

| Network | Type | Data Content | ER Relevance |
|---------|------|-------------|-------------|
| **SWIFT** | Interbank messaging | MT103/MT202 messages, BIC codes, amount, currency | BIC code provides deterministic institution-level entity linkage |
| **Fedwire** | US RTGS | Bank-level transfers, ABA routing numbers | Routing numbers identify US financial institutions; account-level tracing requires subpoena |
| **CHIPS** | US clearing house | Large-value interbank transfers | Network analysis of interbank fund flows |
| **SEPA** | EU payment integration | IBAN-based transfers across 36 countries | IBAN encodes country, bank, and account — deterministic entity linkage signal |
| **Correspondent banking** | Cross-border | Nostro/vostro accounts, intermediary chains | Multi-hop payment chain analysis for sanctions evasion routing detection |

### 2.3 Trade and Shipping Data

- **Customs declarations**: Harmonized System (HS) codes, declared values, parties, countries of origin/destination — for trade-based money laundering (TBML) detection
- **Bills of lading**: Cargo type, volume, routing, involved parties — cross-validation with customs declarations reveals over/under-invoicing
- **AIS vessel tracking**: Maritime domain awareness; dark activity (AIS gaps >24 hours) as sanctions evasion signal — see [[maritime-logistics-gray-zone]]
- **Port throughput data**: Satellite-derived volume estimates vs. declared trade volumes

### 2.4 Commercial and Open-Source Data

- **Corporate registries**: OpenCorporates (220M+ companies), OpenOwnership, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, OCCRP Aleph — see [[corporate-registry-investigation-osint]]
- **Sanctions lists**: OFAC SDN, EU Consolidated List, UN Sanctions, UK OFSI — name-based screening with false-positive rates up to 95% (SymphonyAI 2025)
- **Adverse media**: PEP screening, negative news, enforcement actions — NLP/sentiment pipelines
- **Data breach corpora**: Credential reuse, exposed PII — cross-correlation with financial account data for identity linkage — see [[data-breach-analysis-osint-identity-linkage]]
- **Property records**: Tax assessor data, deed registrations — financial distress indicators, unexplained wealth correlation — see [[property-records-tax-assessor-osint]]

## 3. Agentic FININT: AI Agents in Financial Crime Investigation

A 2026 emerging paradigm: deploying autonomous AI agents for FININT collection, analysis, and entity resolution. This maps to the broader [[agentic-osint-investigation-pipelines]] framework but with specialized adversarial hardening for financial crime.

### 3.1 Architecture Patterns

**Three-tier agent architecture for FININT:**

1. **Collection Agent** — autonomously queries regulatory databases (FinCEN Query, OpenCorporates, sanctions lists), retrieves adverse media, and ingests structured/unstructured financial data. Must respect irreversibility gating ([[irreversibility-gate]]) for regulatory database queries that may trigger scrutiny.

2. **Analysis Agent** — performs entity resolution across heterogeneous datasets using Fellegi-Sunter probabilistic linkage (see §6), SAR narrative NLP extraction (LLM-based financial NER — Lu & Huo 2025, arXiv:2501.02237 identifies five failure types), and network construction for community detection.

3. **Reporting Agent** — generates SAR narratives, produces intelligence products, and surfaces entity clusters for human analyst review. The hallucination-validation gap (4% single-measurement; Palmieri et al. 2026) requires human-in-the-loop validation for actionable intelligence.

### 3.2 Key Capabilities

- **SAR narrative mining**: LLM extraction of entities, amounts, account numbers, and behavioral patterns from unstructured SAR narratives — replacing manual analyst review of 4M+ SARs/year
- **Automated CTR aggregation**: Structuring detection via temporal aggregation of CTRs across multiple accounts and institutions
- **Graph-based entity resolution**: Agentic GraphRAG for financial crime contexts (Capozzi & Helbing 2026, arXiv:2605.18770 — 97.15% merge precision)
- **Cross-institution pattern detection**: Federated learning for privacy-preserving multi-bank analysis (HybridFL: Khan et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.19207, AMLSim + SWIFT benchmarks)
- **Autonomous SAR drafting**: LLM-generated suspicious activity narratives, reducing analyst burden while maintaining narrative quality

### 3.3 Safety and Verification

Agentic FININT operates in a high-stakes domain where false positives lead to unwarranted scrutiny of individuals and false negatives enable financial crime. Key safety mechanisms:
- **Irreversibility gating**: SAR filing and regulatory flagging must never be autonomous — always human-in-the-loop for final decision
- **Source provenance**: Every entity linkage traced to its source document with confidence score
- **Entity binding verification**: Entity resolution binding checks per Babu & Indukuri (arXiv:2606.30531) — 24-26% wrong-entity rate in tool-augmented agents despite 0% wrong-tool rate
- **Adversarial hypothesis testing**: Explicit consideration of evasion scenarios before concluding illicit activity (see [[counterintelligence-analysis-frameworks]])

- **March 2025**: FinCEN interim final rule suspends BOI reporting for US companies and persons
- **2026**: Foreign reporting companies still required; US shell companies remain opaque
- **Congressional tension**: House Financial Services Committee hearing (May 21, 2026, Carole House testimony) debated CTA effectiveness vs. constitutional challenges
- **Implication**: US corporate opacity creates a safe harbor for domestic shell companies used in sanctions evasion — shifts burden to third-party investigative data

### 4.2 AMLA (Anti-Money Laundering Act) Implementation
- **FRAML convergence**: Fraud and AML operations merging at the data layer; ~70% of money laundering cases have fraud as predicate offense (Orbograph 2026)
- **NMLRA 2026**: US Treasury National Money Laundering Risk Assessment (5th edition) — detection methods shift toward graph-based intelligence analysis
- **Real estate transparency**: FinCEN proposed rule (Feb 2024, finalized 2026) requiring real estate professionals to report all-cash residential purchases — closes the luxury real estate money laundering vector

## 5. Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML) Detection

TBML is the dominant method for moving illicit funds across borders — estimated at hundreds of billions annually (FATF-Egmont 2020). Detection requires multi-source alternative data integration.

### 5.1 TBML Typologies (FATF-Egmont 2020)
- **Over/under-invoicing**: Goods declared at prices 20-200% above/below market — requires unit price databases
- **Phantom shipments**: Paperwork exists but no goods move — requires customs-shipping cross-validation
- **Multiple invoicing**: Same goods invoiced multiple times — requires invoice deduplication across data sources
- **Shell company trade**: Goods routed through opaque intermediaries — requires beneficial ownership tracing

### 5.2 Detection Data Requirements
| Data Source | What It Detects | Availability |
|------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Customs declarations** | HS code, declared value, parties | Government-restricted; some commercial aggregators (Panjiva, ImportGenius) |
| **Bills of lading** | Actual cargo, routing, volume | Commercial shipping databases (IHS Markit, Clarksons) |
| **AIS vessel tracking** | Physical movement of goods | Open (MarineTraffic, exactEarth) and commercial |
| **Unit price databases** | Fair market value for declared goods | Commercial (GTA, Comtrade); often country/commodity-specific |
| **Corporate registries** | Beneficial ownership of trading entities | Mixed — UK open, US opaque, offshore jurisdictions closed |

### 5.3 TBML SAR Statistics (Cleareye 2026)
- TBML-related SARs represent only 2% of total fentanyl-related SARs but 42% of aggregate value
- Disproportionate value concentration indicates TBML is the primary mechanism for high-value illicit transfers
- Detection rate remains low due to data fragmentation across customs, shipping, and financial institutions

## 6. Privacy-Preserving FININT Architecture

Multi-institution FININT requires sharing sensitive financial data across banks, FIUs, and law enforcement — creating a fundamental tension between privacy and detection capability.

### 6.1 Privacy Technologies for FININT

| Technology | Application | Maturity |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Federated Learning** | Multi-bank SAR/CTR pattern detection without sharing raw data | TRL 5-6; HybridFL (Khan et al. 2026) demonstrated on AMLSim + SWIFT benchmarks |
| **Homomorphic Encryption** | Encrypted computation over sensitive financial data | See [[homomorphic-encryption-state-of-art]] — 2026 breakthroughs in FHE-LLMs and encrypted GNN inference |
| **Secure Multi-Party Computation** | Joint entity resolution across institutions without exposing individual records | TRL 4-5; PPRL frameworks (REAEDP, ISE_PPRL) adapted for financial entity resolution |
| **Differential Privacy** | Public release of aggregate FININT statistics without individual exposure | See [[differential-privacy-practical-applications]] — \u03b5 calibration for financial crime use-case |
| **Trusted Execution Environments** | Confidential computing for sensitive FININT workloads | TRL 7-8; Intel SGX, AMD SEV for financial analytics |

### 6.2 FinCEN Query System (FCQ)
- BSA data accessible to authorized agencies via FCQ
- IRS Criminal Investigation: ~2M FCQ queries in FY23 (Quantexa 2025)
- 87% of IRS-CI investigations involve BSA filing on primary subject
- Privacy limitation: FCQ access restricted to law enforcement; cross-institution sharing requires legal gateways

### 6.3 The Financial Data Fragmentation Problem

Each institution sees only its transaction slice — a single bank may hold only 5-10% of a money launderer's total transaction volume. Detection requires aggregating signals across institutions without compromising customer privacy. Federated learning + SMPC architectures are the 2026 research frontier.

## 7. Entity Resolution for FININT

FININT alternative data integration is fundamentally an entity resolution problem — melding corporate registries, transaction data, shipping records, and adverse media into a unified investigative graph.

### 7.1 FININT-Specific Entity Resolution Challenges
1. **Adversarial obfuscation**: Deliberate name variations, phonetic substitutions, address manipulation
2. **Jurisdictional fragmentation**: Different identifier systems (SSN/EIN/NINO/Steuer-ID) across countries
3. **Shell company layering**: Multi-jurisdictional ownership chains with deliberate opacity
4. **Temporal resolution**: Entity identity changes over time (name changes, corporate restructuring)
5. **Volume scaling**: 25M+ BSA filings/year with multi-million record cross-matching

### 7.2 Matching Techniques for FININT
| Technique | Application | Example |
|-----------|------------|--------|
| **Soundex/Double Metaphone** | Phonetic name matching across spelling variations | "Smith" \u2194 "Smyth" \u2194 "Smythe" |
| **Alias Resolution** | Known aliases, name variants, transliterations | "Vladimir" \u2194 "Volodymyr" \u2194 "Wladimir" |
| **Date of Birth Fuzzy Matching** | Year match, transposed month/day, \u00b1range | 1985-06-15 \u2194 1985-15-06 \u2194 1985 |
| **Address Standardization** | USPS CASS, international format normalization | "123 Main St" \u2194 "123 Main Street" |
| **Tax ID/SSN/EIN Matching** | Exact + check-digit validation | SSN xxx-xx-xxxx format |
| **Account Number Matching** | IBAN/SWIFT/BIC normalization + routing validation | GB29NWBK60161331926819 \u2192 bank + sort + account |
| **Phone Number Normalization** | E.164 formatting, country code parsing | (212) 555-1234 \u2194 +1-212-555-1234 |

### 7.3 Fellegi-Sunter Integration
FININT entity resolution maps to Fellegi-Sunter probabilistic record linkage:
- **Agreement weights** (high): tax ID, bank account number
- **Agreement weights** (medium): DOB+name combination, phone number
- **Agreement weights** (low): address alone
- **Disagreement weights** (strong evidence against match): SSN mismatch
- **Blocking keys**: ZIP5+last name, IBAN prefix+account type, SWIFT BIC+country code
- **Splink implementation**: Fellegi-Sunter with term frequency adjustments accounts for common surname frequencies in financial data

See [[financial-intelligence-entity-resolution]] (STABLE, 172 lines) for detailed ER methodology.

## 9. Intelligence Failure Patterns in FININT

FININT investigation failures (missed sanctions evasion, undetected TBML) mirror canonical intelligence failure patterns — cognitive closure, mirror-imaging, groupthink (see [[intelligence-failure-analysis]]). Key failure modes specific to FININT:
- **Silo bias**: Each institution sees only its transaction slice; money laundering crosses boundaries undetected
- **Screening fatigue**: False-positive overload from name-based sanctions matching desensitizes analysts — BPI (2020) found true-positive rates below 5% for traditional screening
- **Temporal blind spots**: Quarterly reporting cycles miss real-time evasion activity — the BSA filing lag (30-60 days) creates a structural detection gap
- **Mirror-imaging**: Assuming criminals use sophisticated methods when simple structuring often suffices
- **Groupthink**: Institutional consensus that existing controls are adequate despite evidence of evasion

Counterintelligence dimension: Sophisticated financial criminals employ counter-FININT techniques — transaction structuring below reporting thresholds, shell company layering across opaque jurisdictions, and exploitation of data fragmentation across institutions. These mirror intelligence collection denial and deception (D&D) tactics. See [[counterintelligence-analysis-frameworks]] for CI-ACH methodology applied to adversarial FININT analysis.
