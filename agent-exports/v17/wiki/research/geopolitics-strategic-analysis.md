## US-China Semiconductor Supply Chain

The 2026 MATCH Act (introduced April 2026) extends semiconductor export controls beyond advanced nodes to older-generation DUV lithography and etching tools, directly impacting ASML, Tokyo Electron, and Lam Research equipment exports—potentially affecting 15% of ASML's China revenue. SMIC has achieved volume production of a 5nm-class process (N+3, used in Huawei's Kirin 9030) using multi-patterning with DUV lithography, despite yields of only 30–40% (subsidized by the Chinese government). SMIC and Huawei are reportedly targeting a 3nm gate-all-around (GAA) tape-out in 2026. TSMC's Arizona buildout ($165 B) provides geographical diversification but no organizational redundancy. China aims to increase 7 nm/5 nm output fivefold in two years, while simultaneously developing domestic EUV laser-driven plasma sources. The export control landscape is a layered stack: US BIS rules, Japan's 23-item equipment list, Netherlands EUV/DUV restrictions, and China's retaliatory designations of Japanese companies as controlled entities.

## Rare Earth Supply Chains

China controls ~60% of rare earth mining but ~90% of global processing and refining, creating a critical midstream bottleneck. Heavy rare earth elements—dysprosium (Dy) and terbium (Tb)—are indispensable for defense platforms (e.g., F‑35 contains over 900 lbs of REEs). China's December 2025 export controls on REE processing technology triggered emergency stockpiling among allied nations. Recycling technologies (hydrogen decrepitation, hydrometallurgy, advanced separation) can recover >90% of magnet materials but remain uneconomical against China's dumping economics. The U.S. DoD awarded $5.1 million for domestic recycling R&D. Western processing projects (MP Materials, Lynas) are attempting to rebuild capacity, though many offtake agreements still lock ore to Chinese processors.

## Sanctions Effectiveness

Economic sanctions against Russia have failed to reduce oil volumes: Urals crude averaged $112.3/bbl in April 2026, 2.5× the updated price cap, while Russia's fossil fuel revenues hit €734 M/day—the highest in 2.5 years. Shadow tankers carried 54% of seaborne crude; Ukrainian drone strikes, not sanctions, drove a 24% month-on-month drop in seaborne volumes. For Iran, the U.S. is shifting from entity-level designations to network mapping of evasion ecosystems, sanctioning 19 vessels in May 2026; enforcement has not yet achieved strategic containment. North Korean crypto laundering reached $2 B in stolen assets in 2025, with DPRK IT worker fraud funneling $800 M through multichain wallet clusters; the 11-nation Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team (MSMT) was launched as a reactive coordination model. Key insight: economic pressure alone fails without full blockade, while kinetic disruption (drone strikes) proves more effective—mirroring historical debates on strategic bombing vs. economic coercion in WWII.
