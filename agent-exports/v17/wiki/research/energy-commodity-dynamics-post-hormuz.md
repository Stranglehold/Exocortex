## Overview

Analysis of global energy commodity market dynamics in the aftermath of the February 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis — the largest oil supply disruption in history. Cumulative supply loss reached ~782 million barrels by May 8 and projected to exceed 1 billion barrels by end of May 2026. The crisis removed ~20% of global oil supply, surging Brent to peaks above $115/bbl, triggering record SPR drawdowns, reshaping OPEC+ governance, and accelerating LNG infrastructure buildout.

## 2. OPEC+ in Crisis: Symbolic Hikes, Structural Shifts

### UAE Departure (May 1, 2026)
The UAE's exit from OPEC is the first major producer departure since Qatar (2019) and Ecuador (2020). Removes the second-largest Persian Gulf producer from the quota framework, shifting internal power dynamics.

### June 2026 Output Decision
- **188,000 b/d production increase** agreed by 7 remaining members (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan, Oman)
- **Symbolic increase** — Saudi Arabia's quota rose to 10.291 million bpd but actual March production was only 7.76 million bpd
- Most members cannot meet targets due to Hormuz disruption restricting tanker traffic
- **Brent crude ~$108/bbl** at time of decision; IEA revised 2026 demand forecast down by 730,000 bpd (net contraction of 80,000 bpd)

### Open Question
Can OPEC+ survive without the UAE, or is this the beginning of broader fragmentation? Russia's output is also falling due to Ukrainian drone strikes on refineries (June 2026).

## 3. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) Crisis

### Record Drawdown
- **172 million barrels released** from US SPR (DOE announcement 2026)
- IEA coordinated emergency release: **>400 million barrels** from government stockpiles globally, of which **301 million barrels are crude oil** (Brookings, May 22, 2026)
- This amounts to ~2.5 mb/d buffer over a four-month period
- **"SPR Borrowers Owe Uncle Sam 40 Million Extra Barrels"** — June 2026 reports indicate SPR loan repayment obligations compounding the drawdown
- US SPR now at historically low levels; the reserve was already depleted from 638M bbl (2021 peak) to ~375M bbl pre-crisis

### Temporary Buffer Exhaustion Timeline (Brookings, May 2026)

| Buffer Type | Volume | Effective Rate | Exhaustion Date |
|------------|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Structural: Pipeline bypass (SA East-West + UAE Fujairah) | 5.7 mb/d | Permanent | N/A — structural offset |
| Structural: Pre-war surplus | 0.7 mb/d | Permanent | N/A |
| Temporary: Russian floating storage | ~90M bbl | 1.6 mb/d (avg) | End of April 2026 |
| Temporary: Iranian floating storage | ~60M bbl | 1.3 mb/d (avg) | End of May 2026 |
| Temporary: IEA emergency release (crude) | 301M bbl | 2.5 mb/d | **Mid-July 2026** |

**Critical finding:** By mid-July 2026, all temporary buffers are exhausted. The residual market adjustment is 7.1 mb/d — ~16% of global crude oil trade — that must be absorbed through demand destruction alone. This timeline represents the point of maximum vulnerability for global oil prices.

### Strategic Implications
- At what SPR level does the US lose strategic deterrence capability?
- SPR was designed for 90-day full import replacement; current levels may fall below the operational minimum
- SPR acts as both physical buffer and psychological backstop; depletion of both is accelerating

## 4. US Shale Breakeven Economics

### Permian Basin Dynamics
- Dallas Fed Energy Survey Q1 2026: breakeven prices by basin
- Sustained $90+ WTI should incentivize production growth, but structural constraints persist
- **US crude production:** 13.5 mb/d in 2026, projected 13.8 mb/d in 2027 (EIA April STEO)
- Production growth constrained by: capital discipline (Wall Street demands returns, not volume), Permian sweet spot depletion, permitting bottlenecks, service sector capacity

### Structural Constraints
- Shale industry consolidation (Exxon/Pioneer, Chevron/Hess, Conoco/Marathon) concentrates decision-making in fewer hands — majors prioritize shareholder returns over growth
- DUC (drilled but uncompleted) well inventory depleted; new drilling required for incremental barrels
- Federal leasing policy uncertainty under Trump administration's "Drill Baby Drill 2.0" with Arctic Refuge lease auction opened June 2026

## 5. US LNG Export Boom

### Terminal Buildout
- US LNG export capacity: **17 Bcf/d** in 2026, projected **19 Bcf/d** in 2027 (EIA)
- March 2026 exports hit **~18 Bcf/d**, near record December 2025 peak
- Only very limited flexibility exists to increase exports — deferred maintenance, new project ramp-ups, recent export authorization agreements
- Henry Hub prices expected to remain moderate ($3.67/MBtu in 2026) despite global LNG price spikes due to export capacity constraints

### Geopolitical Implications
- US LNG replaces lost Qatari and UAE volumes in European and Asian markets
- Spread between US Henry Hub and European/Asian import prices has sharply increased
- South Korea locking in Canadian crude and LNG in sweeping supply overhaul (June 2026)
- Pakistan rushing to secure LNG as summer power demand surges
- Australian LNG strike at Ichthys disrupting cargo loadings — compounding global supply tightness

## 6. JP Morgan Warning: From Crude Crisis to Refining Crisis

JP Morgan's head of global commodities strategy Natasha Kaneva (May 2026):
- Commercial oil inventories in the developed world could "approach operational stress levels" by June 2026
- If Hormuz does not reopen: "The next phase of this shock may look less like a traditional crude spike and more like a refining and end-user fuel crisis"
- Only a "clear, credible announcement" of strait reopening averts the shortage scenario

**Diesel prices:** EIA projects $5.80/gal peak in April 2026, averaging $4.80/gal for the year — elevated due to tight global supplies and US inventories below five-year average.
