## What I Found

### Oil Market Glut (2026-2027)
- **EIA Projection**: 2.1M bbl/day inventory buildup in 2026, peaking at 2.7M bbl/day in Q1
- **IEA Projection**: 4M bbl/day imbalance by 2026 — "increasingly clear that something has to give"
- **Brent Price**: Projected at $52/bbl by close of 2025, $62/bbl by end of 2026
- **WTI**: Dipped below $60/bbl in mid-October 2025, recovering to $70+ after Middle East tensions
- **China Stockpiling**: 140M bbl added to storage March-June 2026 (Rystad: "huge number")

### OPEC+ Dynamics
- **Cuts**: 2.2M bbl/day voluntary cuts scheduled to unwind by 2027
- **Enforcement**: Preexisting bottlenecks may slow unwinding — EIA noted this
- **Russia Offset**: OPEC+ signaled other members could offset any Russian export shortfall

### Russia Sanctions
- **Refining Disruption**: 38% of Russia's 6.5M bbl/day refining capacity disrupted by Ukraine drone strikes
- **Production**: Upstream production sites untouched — barrels still flowing to foreign ports
- **Sanctions Evasion**: Sanctioned barrels repeatedly finding their way to global markets

### US Shale
- **Record Production**: 13.6M bbl/day in July 2026 (EIA)
- **Breakeven Trajectory**: Marginal cost rising from ~$70/bbl WTI today to ~$95/bbl by mid-2030s
- **Acreage Quality**: Best acreage exhausted, moving to lower-quality rock

### LNG Market
- **US Capacity**: To rise by 5 Bcf/D by 2026, total export capacity ~16 Bcf/D
- **Henry Hub**: Near $3.40/MMBtu in late October 2025 (+50% YoY)
- **EIA Forecast**: Prices to top $4/MMBtu by January 2026
- **Competition**: US, Middle East, and Africa LNG buildout could translate to softer prices beyond 2026

## What I Think Is Interesting

The **2026 oil market glut** is the most significant finding. Three potential correction paths:
1. OPEC+ reverses course and reinstates production cuts
2. Western sanctions enforcement tightens, curbing Russian/Iranian exports
3. US shale producers slow growth in response to weaker margins

The **Russia situation** is particularly complex: 38% refining capacity disrupted, but upstream production untouched. This means Russian barrels are "as eager as ever to find their way to foreign ports" — sanctions are creating a parallel market with price discounts, not eliminating supply.

The **LNG opportunity** is a second-order effect: with oil prices under pressure, gas becomes relatively more attractive. US LNG export growth (+5 Bcf/D by 2026) and Henry Hub price trajectory ($3.40 → $4/MMBtu) suggest a shift in global gas trade patterns.
