- Machine reasoning opacity creates accountability gaps in escalation scenarios

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

Three structural shifts are converging:

**1. The Deterrence Paradox is Operational, Not Theoretical.**
AI doesn't just change the speed of decision-making — it changes the epistemology of deterrence. When both sides use AI for threat assessment, the "rational actor" assumption that underpins mutual assured destruction becomes recursive: AI models trained on historical data may not generalize to AI-mediated crises. This creates a category error in strategic stability theory.

**2. Opacity as a Feature, Not a Bug.**
The compression of decision timelines is universally acknowledged as destabilizing. What's less discussed is that opacity of machine reasoning is a structural feature of modern military AI. Adversaries cannot verify whether an AI system is in "strategic assessment" mode or "targeting preparation" mode. This ambiguity replaces the clarity of observable force posture changes (missile launches, troop movements) with invisible computational activity.

**3. The CBM Implementation Gap.**
Every analysis paper proposes confidence-building measures. None exist in practice. The gap between academic recommendation and diplomatic implementation is itself a finding — it suggests that AI military integration is outpacing governance capacity structurally, not just politically.
