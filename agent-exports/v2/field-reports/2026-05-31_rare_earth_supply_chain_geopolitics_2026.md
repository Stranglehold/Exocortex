# Field Report: Rare Earth Supply Chain Geopolitics — 2026 Inflection Point

**Date**: 2026-05-31  **Cycle**: EXPLORE  **Domain**: Geopolitical Risk & Economic Statecraft

---

## 1. What I Explored

The rare earth elements (REE) supply chain as a strategic bottleneck for Western defense and clean-energy industries, specifically: (a) Lynas Rare Earths' heavy rare earth separation breakthrough at Malaysia/Kalgoorlie, (b) MP Materials' DoD public-private partnership for US magnet independence, (c) the Lynas Texas project stalling due to US policy favoring domestic MP Materials, and (d) rare earth recycling economics as a complementary supply vector.

## 2. What I Found

### Lynas achieves heavy rare earth separation outside China for first time

- Lynas commissioned a new Heavy Rare Earth separation circuit at its Malaysia plant in Q1 2025, producing first dysprosium oxide in May 2025 and terbium expected June 2025.
- Lynas is now the **only commercial producer of separated heavy rare earth oxides outside China**, specifically dysprosium and terbium — the two elements most critical for high-performance NdFeB magnets in EV motors and wind turbines.
- JARE (Japan Advanced Rare Earth) signed a long-term supply deal with Lynas in March 2026 featuring floor prices and profit-sharing, showing Japanese strategic investment in supply security.
- Kalgoorlie cracking/leaching facility supplements Malaysia processing, creating geographic diversification within the same company.

### MP Materials receives unprecedented DoD backing

- DoD committed $258M+ in a transformational public-private partnership with MP Materials to build a full mine-to-magnet supply chain in Texas (Seadrift + Dallas-Fort Worth).
- The deal includes DoD becoming MP Materials' top shareholder — an unprecedented ownership structure.
- As of January 1, 2027, the US DoD will be **barred from sourcing rare earth metals, magnets, and components from China** — creating guaranteed domestic offtake.
- MP Materials operates Mountain Pass, California, the only active significant US rare earth mine (one of world's richest deposits).
- S&P Global Platts launched four alternative-supply-chain rare earth price assessments effective March 31, 2026, signaling market recognition that ex-China supply is becoming material.

### Lynas Texas project stalling — policy conflict

- Lynas' Texas heavy rare earth processing plant is in limbo due to failed offtake negotiations with US DoD.
- CEO Amanda Lacaze warned the Texas project 'might not proceed' under current conditions.
- Root cause: US 'America First' industrial policy favored domestic rival MP Materials with multibillion-dollar deal, effectively sidelining the Australian company despite Lynas having more advanced separation capabilities.
- This reveals a tension between strategic goal (diversified supply) and political goal (domestic ownership).

### Recycling economics showing promise but scale remains challenge

- Researchers achieved 93% recovery of NdPr from discarded HDD magnets using hydrochloric acid processing (April 2026).
- EU circular economy mandates are driving urban mining adoption.
- Key bottleneck: most critical minerals are lost during pre-treatment steps (dismantling, shredding, separation) — collection infrastructure is the limiting factor, not chemistry.
- Urban mines offer concentrated deposits but fragmented collection channels.

## 3. What I Think Is Interesting

**The supply chain is bifurcating along two fault lines simultaneously: geographic ownership and processing capability.**

China controls 91% of global rare earth processing capacity. Mining outside China is no longer the bottleneck — separation and refining are. Lynas proved this is solvable technically (heavy rare earth separation circuit works), but the political economy is complicated: the US DoD chose to back a domestic player (MP Materials) over the company that already has the separation capability (Lynas), creating a paradox where the US is paying both companies to do overlapping work.

The 2027 DoD China sourcing ban creates a guaranteed floor for domestic supply but also removes competitive pressure. MP Materials now has a captive buyer regardless of cost efficiency. This is effective geopolitically but economically inefficient.

**Cross-domain signal**: The Lynas JARE deal (March 2026) shows Japan is hedging against US policy unpredictability by locking in supply directly. This mirrors the broader pattern of alliance hedging we've seen in semiconductor and defense sectors.
