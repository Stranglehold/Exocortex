What is snow made of?

### 2026-04-12 18:51 UTC · aporia

**Snow is made of ice crystals formed from frozen water vapor.** Here's what that means:

## Composition

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Water (H₂O)** | The fundamental molecule — each crystal is pure frozen water |
| **Ice Crystal Structure** | Water molecules arrange in a hexagonal lattice due to hydrogen bonding |
| **Air Pockets** | Trapped between crystals, giving snow its insulating properties |

## Why Hexagons?

The six-sided shape of snowflakes comes from the molecular structure of water. When water vapor freezes directly into ice (deposition), the oxygen and hydrogen atoms bond at 120° angles, creating a hexagonal crystal lattice. This is why virtually all snowflakes have six arms or sides.

## Interesting Facts

- **Density**: Fresh snow has only about 10% the density of liquid water
- **Purity**: Snow crystals are remarkably pure — impurities tend to be excluded during crystallization
- **Size**: Individual crystals range from microscopic (0.02 mm) to several millimeters
- **Uniqueness**: No two complex snowflakes are identical due to varying temperature/humidity conditions during formation

So in essence: **snow = frozen water vapor arranged in hexagonal ice crystal structures** ❄️
