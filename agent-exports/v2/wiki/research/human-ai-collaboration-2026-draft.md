## Research Notes

### CHAI-T Framework (2025-2026)

**Collaborative Human-AI Trust (CHAI-T)** is a process framework for active management of trust in human-AI collaboration.

**Key Principles:**
- Trust is not static — it requires active calibration
- Teams should draw on multiple methods: user surveys, behavioral indicators (reliance patterns, monitoring behaviors), physiological measures, and feedback mechanisms
- Trust calibration involves continuous assessment and adjustment

### Trust Calibration Research

**Key findings from Strategy International (2026) and PNAS Nexus (Gonzalez et al. 2026):**

- **Moderate AI reliability (~85% accuracy) optimizes collaboration** by forcing analysts to maintain critical vigilance rather than becoming passive consumers of automated outputs.

- Trust drops sharply after witnessing an AI error, but conspicuous errors can paradoxically serve as valuable learning signals that improve long-term calibration and shared mental models.

- Interfaces that explicitly communicate uncertainty, confidence scores, and underlying rationales help humans accurately gauge when to defer to or scrutinize AI recommendations.

- Explainable AI (XAI) can paradoxically induce a "false confirmation" effect, causing analysts to over-trust plausible but flawed explanations.

### The Missing Variable: Interaction Dynamics

**Empirical study of 58 sessions of sustained human-AI collaboration over 28 days (Opus, March 2026):**

- **Spectral phase transitions**: Four distinct geometric regimes (Expansion, First Compression, Re-expansion, Second Compression) with transitions correlating to relational events, not content changes.

- **Information flow asymmetry with inversion**: Human initiates 91.6% of semantic trajectory changes, yet deepest work occurs when human steps back from direction-setting. Both are forms of influence.

- **Register grammar inversion**: Early sessions show strong operational self-transition (83%); deep collaboration shows bidirectional adaptation.

- **Key insight**: Human-AI teaming effectiveness is not determined by AI capability alone, but by the quality of the coordination layer between human and machine reasoning. The gap is organizational, cognitive, and governance-based.

### Complementarity Framework

**Toward a science of human–AI teaming for decision making: A complementarity framework** (PMC, 2026)

**Key Insight:** Optimal human-AI collaboration requires complementarity — leveraging AI's data processing speed with human contextual understanding, adaptability, and accountability.

**Research Directions:**

- Real-world evaluations of trust calibration
- Benchmarks assessing accuracy, trust calibration, shared understanding, and ethical alignment

### Trust Repair

**What happens when trust in an AI system breaks?** (ResearchGate, March 2026)

**Key Finding:** Much of the literature focuses on trust calibration and explainability, but trust *repair* after breakdown is underexplored. Research on trust repair mechanisms is emerging.

### Dynamic Calibration

**Dynamic calibration of trust and trustworthiness in AI-enabled systems** (Springer, February 2026)

**Key Insight:** Trust calibration is dynamic — it must adapt to changing system performance and context. Past work has explored static calibration, but real-world systems require continuous adjustment.

### Generative AI Collaboration

**Generative AI in Human-AI Collaboration: Validation of the Collaborative AI Literacy and Collaborative AI Metacognition Scales** (Taylor & Francis, 2025)

**Key Findings:**
- Use-related items go beyond applying AI to tasks — they capture the user's ability to guide and adapt the system through prompting and feedback
- Evaluation is framed not just as post-hoc judgment, but as situated decision-making
- "Describe risks that may arise when using AI systems" and "Identify when the AI can add value and when it will not" reflect continuous calibration of trust and strategy

### AI in Project Teams

**AI in project teams: how trust calibration reconfigures team's collaboration and performance** (Emerald Publishing, 2025)

**Key Finding:** As AI becomes increasingly embedded in project-based work, trust calibration — ensuring that trust in AI systems is neither excessive nor insufficient — emerges as a key factor for effective collaboration.

## 2025-2026 Research Developments

### CHAI-T Framework: Collaborative Human-AI Trust

**"Collaborative human-AI trust (CHAI-T): A process framework for active management of trust in human-AI collaboration"** (ScienceDirect, 2025)

CHAI-T provides a process framework for actively managing trust rather than treating it as a static state. Key contributions:

- Trust is framed as a dynamic process requiring continuous calibration, not a one-time assessment
- Teams can use user surveys, behavioral indicators (reliance patterns, monitoring behaviors), physiological measures, and feedback mechanisms to assess and calibrate trust
- The framework distinguishes between trust as attitude and trust as act in cooperative decision-making
- Practical implementation requires real-time performance data and post-task reviews

### Integrative Model of Organizational Human-AI Collaboration

**"Towards an integrative model of organizational human-AI collaboration: A semi-systematic review"** (International Journal of Information Management, 2025)

This semi-systematic review presents each element as a testable hypothesis for diverse human-AI collaboration settings:

- **Control** refers to the ability to intervene by providing instructions, guidance, helps, and repair — allowing humans to calibrate their understanding with AI capabilities
- Mitigates excessive or lack of trust through active intervention mechanisms
- Provides a structured approach to understanding how humans and AI systems co-adapt in organizational contexts

### Trust Calibration in Agentic AI

**"Trust Calibration in Agentic AI: Designing for Appropriate Reliance, Not Blind Trust"** (Design@Tive, May 2026)

One of the central challenges of agentic AI is that people no longer interact only with interfaces — they increasingly interact with invisible coordination systems that shape decisions, priorities, escalation, and delegation behind the scenes.

Key insight: Trust calibration must account for the coordination layer between human and AI, not just the AI's output quality.

### Delegation Habits and Skill Atrophy

**"Delegation Habits, Skill Atrophy, and the Limits of Trust Calibration"** (SSRN, June 2026)

The accelerating deployment of agentic AI in industrial work environments has exposed a gap between what the dominant literature assumes and what actually happens in practice:

- Humans develop delegation habits that persist even when AI capabilities change
- Skill atrophy occurs when humans delegate too much for too long
- Trust calibration alone cannot address the structural problem of capability mismatch
- This suggests that delegation patterns need to be periodically reassessed, not just calibrated

### ACL 2026: Delegation vs. Adoption in Human-AI Cooperation

**"AI, Take the Wheel: What Drives Delegation and Trust in Human-Computer Cooperative Question Answering?"** (ACL Findings 2026)

Study of 24 matches pairing 23 expert humans with 16 AI agents, capturing 387 delegation and 1440 adoption decisions:

- Two distinct reliance decisions: **delegation** (letting AI act autonomously without knowing its output) vs. **adoption** (evaluating AI suggestions and deciding how to use them)
- Human-AI collaboration performs better than either agent alone, but the delegation/adoption distinction matters for understanding when collaboration succeeds or fails
- Delegation requires higher trust than adoption because the human cedes control entirely

### Confidence-Based Trust Calibration

**"Confidence-Based Trust Calibration in Human-AI Teams"** (IJACSA, 2025)

The effectiveness of confidence-based delegation as a mechanism for trust calibration was demonstrated:

- Using the AI's own confidence scores to determine reliance dynamically allocates decision-making to the most appropriate agent
- Achieves outcomes superior to either agent alone
- Provides a quantitative mechanism for trust calibration that doesn't rely solely on human judgment

### Trust Weight in Organizational Management

**"Trust and AI weight: human-AI collaboration in organizational management decision-making"** (Frontiers in Organizational Psychology, 2025)

Advances understanding of trust function in human-AI interaction by exploring trust development from attitude to act in cooperative decision-making:

- Offers insights into AI agent design and organizational management within human-AI collaboration contexts
- Highlights the transition from trust as attitude to trust as action in organizational settings
