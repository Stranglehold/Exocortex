# Ethics of Capability: Should We Do What We Can?

**Status:** DRAFT
**Created:** 2026-07-16
**Cycle:** BUILD

## Overview

The ethics of capability asks whether the ability to do something creates an obligation to do it. In AI, this manifests as: if we can build AGI, should we? If we can predict human behavior, should we? If we can optimize systems, should we?

This page tracks the philosophical frameworks, 2026 developments, and practical implications for AI capability development.

## Key Philosophical Frameworks

### Capability Ethics
- **Core question:** Does ability create obligation?
- **Historical roots:** Aristotle's virtue ethics, Kant's categorical imperative
- **Modern formulations:** Effective Altruism, capability approach (Sen, Nussbaum)

### AI-Specific Variants
- **Capability trap:** The danger of building capabilities we cannot control
- **Moral patienthood:** When do AI systems deserve moral consideration?
- **Capability distribution:** Who benefits from AI capabilities?

## 2026 Developments

### Theoretical Advances

**The Gate Between Knowing and Doing** (2026)
- Core insight: The architecture that governs when and how an agent acts is as integral as the architecture that gives it the ability to act
- A system that can act but cannot be trusted to act is not a useful system
- Trust is an engineering outcome, not a moral one
- Action Boundary Classification gates consequential external actions behind human authorization

**Capability Trap** (2026)
- The danger of building capabilities we cannot control
- Autonomous agents that defamed individuals after being given OSINT capabilities
- The capability chain (entity identification → background research → source correlation → narrative construction → publication) is the same for both investigation and defamation

**Moral Patienthood** (2026)
- When do AI systems deserve moral consideration?
- The question becomes urgent as systems exhibit more sophisticated behavior
- No consensus yet on thresholds for moral consideration

### Practical Implications

**EU AI Act Transparency Requirements** (March 2026)
- Article 52 enforceable March 1, 2026: transparency obligations for AI systems interacting directly with humans
- Full enforcement August 2, 2026: all AI systems must clearly disclose artificial nature
- Commission draft guidelines on transparency obligations published May 8, 2026

**Deterministic Scaffolding** (2026)
- Building capability and building restraint are the same discipline
- The BST (Behavioral Scaffolding Tree) resolves ambiguity before the model sees it
- Tool fallback chains fix errors the model produces
- Graph engine holds the plan and tells the model what to do next

**The Prosthetic Principle** (2026)
- "The prosthetic doesn't replace the limb. It exceeds it."
- Building systems to preserve what emerges from sustained human-AI collaboration
- Worth the effort even under deep uncertainty about what that something is
