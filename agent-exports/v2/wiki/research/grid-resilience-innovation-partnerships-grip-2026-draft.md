# DOE Grid Resilience and Innovation Partnerships (GRIP) 2026

## Overview

The Grid Resilience and Innovation Partnerships (GRIP) program represents one of the largest federal investments in grid modernization under the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law (BIL). Administered by the DOE Office of Electricity through the Grid Deployment Office (GDO), GRIP is allocating approximately **$10.5 billion over five years (FY22–FY26)** to enhance grid flexibility and improve resilience against extreme weather, aging infrastructure, evolving cybersecurity threats, and rising load demand.

As of March 2026, the GRIP funding opportunity was renamed to **SPARK** (Strengthening Power & Resilience via Accelerated Knowledge) under the new administration, though existing awards continue under original terms. (Source: UtilityDive, March 13, 2026)

## Funding Structure

GRIP operates through three distinct sub-programs:

| Sub-Program | Purpose | Typical Award Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Grid Resilience Grants** | Prevent outages, deploy technologies for grid flexibility, demonstrate innovative approaches to power sector resilience | $50M–$300M per project |
| **Smart Grid Investment (SGI)** | Deploy advanced grid technologies (AMI, advanced distribution management, grid-edge computing) | $100M–$500M per project |
| **Grid Innovation Program** | State/Tribe/local government collaboration with utility operators to deploy innovative transmission, storage, and distribution infrastructure | Variable, multi-state consortia |

## Award Rounds

### Round 1 (October 2023)
- **Total awarded:** $3.5 billion
- **Projects:** 58 projects across 44 states
- **Focus:** Initial resilience deployments, grid-edge technology demonstrations, transmission upgrades
- **Source:** DOE announcement, Federal Register

### Round 2 (November 2023)
- **Total awarded:** $3.9 billion
- **Projects:** 38 projects across 42 states
- **Capacity enabled:** Upwards of 7.5 GW of new grid infrastructure
- **Focus:** Transmission reconductoring, advanced grid technologies, distribution resilience
- **Source:** DOE announcement, Renewable Energy World, DatacenterDynamics

### Remaining Budget
- Approximately $3.1 billion unallocated as of mid-2026
- Final funding window expected before FY26 closeout
- SPARK rebranding (March 2026) introduced additional focus areas for transmission and grid-edge AI

## Technology Deployment Categories

Analysis of awarded projects reveals six dominant technology categories:

1. **Advanced Distribution Management Systems (ADMS)** — Grid-scale software platforms enabling real-time monitoring, fault detection, and automated reconfiguration
2. **Grid-Edge Computing & AI** — Distributed intelligence at substations and distribution points for predictive maintenance, fault prediction, and automated response
3. **Energy Storage Integration** — Battery energy storage systems (BESS) co-located with renewable generation and at critical distribution nodes
4. **Transmission Reconductoring** — High-capacity conductor upgrades to increase existing right-of-way utilization without new corridors
5. **Microgrid & Islanding Capability** — Local grid segments capable of autonomous operation during bulk grid outages
6. **Cyber-Physical Security** — Hardened protection relay architectures, secure communication channels (IEC 61850 cybersecurity), and threat detection

## Grid-Edge AI Intersection

GRIP projects create significant tailwinds for grid-edge AI deployment:

- **AI for Power Challenge (May 2026):** EPRI-led initiative advancing AI innovation across the energy sector; applications closing mid-2026. Directly complements GRIP-funded deployments by providing AI tooling for grid operators.
- **DOE INL Analysis (March 2026):** "Securing the Modern Grid: Federal Investments, Digitization, and Supply Chain Strategy" documents widespread interest in digital technologies across GRIP-awarded projects, with particular emphasis on AI-driven condition monitoring and predictive maintenance.
- **Transmission-as-constraint framing:** GRIP Round 2 explicitly frames transmission capacity as the bottleneck for AI-driven load growth, linking grid modernization directly to AI datacenter expansion.

### Key Implications for Grid-Edge AI

| Implication | Detail |
|---|---|
| Funding multiplier | GRIP awards typically require 20-30% non-federal match, leveraging additional private capital |
| Technology readiness | Projects must demonstrate TRL 6+ for deployment, favoring proven grid-edge AI solutions |
| Cybersecurity baseline | NERC CIP compliance is prerequisite; AI systems must meet IEC 62351 security standards |
| DER interconnection | IEEE 1547-2018/2023 compliance required for all distributed energy resource integration |

## Implementation Timelines

- **Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) issuance:** Q1 2022 (initial rounds)
- **Application deadlines:** Varied by round; Round 1 closed Q3 2022, Round 2 closed Q4 2023
- **Award announcements:** October 2023 (Round 1), November 2023 (Round 2)
- **Project execution window:** 2024–2027 (typical 3-year deployment cycle)
- **Program closeout:** FY26 (September 2026) for final funding obligation

## Regulatory Framework

GRIP projects operate under multiple regulatory layers:

1. **Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC)** — Jurisdiction over interstate transmission; Order 2222 enables DER aggregation participation in wholesale markets
2. **North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC)** — Mandatory reliability standards (NERC CIP) for bulk power system assets
3. **State Public Utility Commissions (PUCs)** — Rate case approval required for cost recovery; GRIP funding may offset traditional rate-base requirements
4. **DOE Grid Deployment Office (GDO)** — Program administration, milestone tracking, and compliance monitoring

## Key Insight

**GRIP creates a $10.5B deployment pipeline that inherently validates grid-edge AI as infrastructure-grade technology.** The TRL 6+ requirement means AI solutions deployed under GRIP must demonstrate production readiness, effectively raising the bar for what constitutes "grid-ready" AI. The SPARK rebranding and explicit framing of transmission as the AI load-growth bottleneck signals that grid modernization is now directly coupled to AI datacenter expansion planning.
