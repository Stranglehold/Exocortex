## Overview

Geopolitical analysis of global semiconductor manufacturing supply chains. US-China semiconductor decoupling is the dominant strategic competition of 2026, with cascading effects on AI capability, defense systems, and critical infrastructure. Industry experiencing unprecedented transformation — globalized efficiency model fragmenting into regional manufacturing ecosystems with distinct technological trajectories (Omdia Sep 2025).

## US-China Export Control Regime

**BIS Advanced Computing Rules (2022-2026):**
- January 2026 BIS rule revised export license review policy for advanced computing semiconductors, new TPP thresholds and case-by-case review (ECCN Finder 2026)
- Since 2018, US systematically tightened controls to restrict PRC access to advanced chips and AI computing (CRS Report R48642)
- H.R. 4505 Export Controls Enforcement Act and H.R. 7003 BIS STRENGTH Act filed Q1 2026
- MATCH Act introduced April 2026 would extend controls to DUV lithography tools

**Tariff Escalation (2025):**
- US implemented 100% tariffs on semiconductor imports from China (TraxTech 2025)
- Decoupling risks: ITIF Nov 2025 warns export controls reduce US chipmaker revenues, lower R&D investment, reduce employment

**Waiver Revocations (2025):**
- US revoked TSMC Nanjing waiver requiring new export licenses (TechRepublic 2025)
- TSMC, SK Hynix, Samsung lost waivers for exporting chipmaking equipment to China plants (CNBC Sep 2025)
- Taiwan implemented own semiconductor export controls targeting China chip giants (TechWireAsia Jun 2025)

## TSMC Arizona Expansion

- Total investment scaled to $465B across 11 coordinated fab phases per US-Taiwan tariff framework (Apr 2026)
- CHIPS Act subsidy: $6.6B finalized award from Dept of Commerce
- CHIPS Act allocated $52.7B total; TSMC received largest share
- Global semiconductor capacity 2026: $975B industry growing 26% driven by AI demand

## SMIC Capabilities & China Self-Sufficiency

- SMIC demonstrated 7nm and achieved 5nm-class chips via DUV multi-patterning (SAQP) at ~20% yield (AEI Apr 2026)
- 1.6M dies targeted for 2026 production run
- China testing first domestically-built DUV machine at SMIC
- Self-sufficiency rate: 20% (2022) → projected 45% by 2027; state target 80% by 2030
- China investing $47.5B domestic fund for semiconductor independence (2025)
- Limitations: DUV multi-patterning for 5nm significantly more expensive/lower yield than EUV

## Equipment Restrictions

- EUV export ban fully enforced; ASML cannot sell EUV to China
- MATCH Act would extend controls to DUV lithography
- China cannot domestically produce advanced lithography at commercial scale
- Affected manufacturers: ASML (Netherlands), Tokyo Electron (Japan), Lam Research (US)

## Rare Earth Materials Dependency

**China's Dominance:**
- China controls 69% of global rare earth production and 90% of processing
- October 2025: China dramatically expanded rare earth export controls, adding 5 new elements and extra scrutiny for semiconductor/defense users (Reuters Oct 2025)
- Export restrictions on critical elements enforced since 2025

**Western Alternatives:**
- MP Materials (Mountain Pass, CA) expanding into integrated processing
- Lynas Rare Earths (Australia/Malaysia) leading non-China supplier
- Critical gap: Mountain Pass ore has only traces of heavy rare earths (Dy, Tb)
- Heavy rare earth gap remains most vulnerable Western supply chain point

## Allied Coordination

- Netherlands (ASML): aligned on EUV restrictions, DUV tightening
- Japan (Tokyo Electron): coordinating equipment export controls
- South Korea: exposed — Korean chipmakers with Chinese fabs affected
- Australia (Lynas): critical rare earth processing alternative
- Quad alliance (US/India/Japan/Australia): considering semiconductor cooperation (Modern Diplomacy Mar 2025)

## Strategic Assessment

1. US/Taiwan/Japan/Netherlands coalition enforcing export control regime
2. China accelerating domestic substitution with mixed results (7nm feasible, 5nm marginal, EUV impossible without ASML)
3. Rare earth dependency remains China's most potent leverage; Oct 2025 escalation targets defense/semiconductor users
4. TSMC Arizona represents single largest industrial policy investment in US history
5. Supply chain bifurcating into two competing technological ecosystems ending decades of globalization (Omdia 2025)
