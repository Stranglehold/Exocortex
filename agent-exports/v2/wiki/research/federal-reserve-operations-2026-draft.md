## Deepening (2026-07-18)

### Balance Sheet Trajectory

**QT Timeline (June 2022 – December 2025):**
- Peak balance sheet: ~$9 trillion (2022)
- QT pace: $60B/month Treasury + $35B/month MBS ($95B/month total)
- Reduced to $60B/month Treasury + $35B/month MBS (April 2022)
- Further reduced to $25B/month Treasury + $15B/month MBS (June 2022)
- Final QT pace: $20B/month Treasury + $15B/month MBS ($35B/month total)
- **QT ended December 1, 2025** after 3.5 years of balance sheet runoff
- Total reduction: ~$2.5 trillion (from $9T to ~$6.5T)

**Why QT Ended Early:**
- Money market liquidity conditions began tightening as reserves approached the 'efficient and effective' threshold
- Federal funds rate volatility increased near the target range floor (Reuters, Oct 29 2025)
- ON RRP drawdown to near-zero removed the primary shock absorber for balance sheet runoff
- St. Louis Fed estimated ample reserves at ~$3.2T; actual reserves had declined below this by Q4 2025
- Primary dealer balance sheet constraints became binding (RepoMech arXiv:2512.23842)

**Reserve Level Thresholds:**
- FOMC determined reserves had declined to 'efficient and effective' levels by December 2025
- Ample reserves estimate: ~$3.2 trillion (St. Louis Fed, February 2026)
- Below this level, the ample reserves regime becomes less effective for monetary policy implementation
- This represents a permanent change to the monetary policy toolkit

### Repo Market Mechanics

**Repo Market Structure:**
- Overnight repo market: ~$2-3 trillion daily volume
- Term repo market: provides funding stability for dealers
- Standing Repo Facility (SRF): acts as a backstop for short-term funding stress
- Money market fund flows shifted from ON RRP to direct Treasury exposure

**Post-2019 Stress Evolution:**
- September 2019 repo spike exposed structural fragility in funding markets
- Fed responded with $1.5T in repo operations over 3 weeks
- Standing Repo Facility established as permanent backstop
- ON RRP facility created to provide a floor for the federal funds rate
- Money market fund dynamics fundamentally altered by these facilities

**Dealer Balance Sheet Constraints:**
- Primary dealers face regulatory capital constraints (LCR, NSFR)
- Balance sheet capacity limits Treasury market making
- RepoMech framework (arXiv:2512.23842) proposes reducing balance-sheet impact of repo operations
- Dealer financing costs rose as excess buffer eroded

### Treasury Market Functioning

**Treasury Issuance and Debt Management:**
- Treasury General Account (TGA) dynamics affect reserve levels
- TGA refill during QT pulled reserves from banking system simultaneously with QT
- Primary dealer system provides market making for Treasury securities
- Term premium dynamics influenced by Fed balance sheet operations

**Treasury Market Resilience:**
- Treasury market showed resilience despite three years of QT
- Banking exchange noted the shift marks a 'critical inflection point in reserve availability and funding costs'
- Interagency Working Group on Treasury Market Surveillance (2022) identified structural vulnerabilities

### Asymmetric Balance Sheet Policy

**Post-QT Framework:**
- Post-QT, the Fed's balance sheet is effectively a ceiling-only instrument
- Future tightening must come through interest rate adjustments rather than balance sheet reduction
- $3.2T reserve level appears to be a structural floor below which ample reserves regime loses effectiveness
- This represents a permanent change to the monetary policy toolkit

**Forward Guidance and Communication:**
- Fed communications significantly affect market expectations
- FOMC statements and dot plot provide guidance on future policy path
- Forward guidance has become increasingly important as balance sheet tools are exhausted
