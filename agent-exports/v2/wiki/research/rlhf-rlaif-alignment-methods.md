## Overview
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) for language model alignment. Covers the evolution from PPO-based RLHF through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and post-RLHF methods (KTO, ORPO, SimPO), reward model contamination risks, and alignment tax quantification.

## RLHF Pipeline (Baseline)

### Classical InstructGPT Pipeline (RLHF + PPO)
- **Stage 1**: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality instruction data
- **Stage 2**: Reward Model (RM) training on human preference pairs via Bradley-Terry modeling
- **Stage 3**: PPO fine-tuning of policy model against frozen RM with KL penalty to reference model
- **PPO instability**: 4-model architecture (policy, reference, reward, value), training divergence common, hyperparameter-sensitive (lr, clip range, KL coeff)
- **arXiv 2404.10719**: Comprehensive DPO vs PPO study across dialogue and code generation testbeds. PPO achieves higher peak performance in challenging code competitions but DPO is more stable and simpler to deploy.

## Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Era

### DPO (Rafailov et al. 2023)
- Mathematically equivalent to RLHF reward maximization under Bradley-Terry assumptions, but eliminates explicit reward model training and PPO loop
- Single-model optimization: loss directly parameterized over chosen/rejected pairs
- **Production advantage**: 2x fewer models to maintain, eliminates reward model training stage, more stable convergence
- **Limitation**: requires paired preference data; struggles with ambiguous or noisy labels

### DPO Variants (2024-2026)
- **IPO (Identity Preference Optimization)** — individual fairness regularization; better for per-instance calibration but higher compute
- **KTO (Kahneman-Tversky Optimization)** — operates on unlabeled preference pairs using K-T psychological model; useful when paired data unavailable
- **ORPO (Odds Ratio Preference Optimization)** — odds-ratio parameterization, improved gradient stability on imbalanced preference datasets
- **SimPO (arXiv 2405.14734, NeurIPS 2024)** — reference-free reward using average log probability length normalization; simpler than DPO, competitive on MT-Bench and AlpacaEval
- **alpha-DPO (OpenReview 2025)** — adaptive reward margin tuning based on sample difficulty
- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)** — group-level preference aggregation for multi-turn dialogue alignment

### DPO vs PPO in Production
- **arXiv 2404.10719**: PPO achieves higher ceiling on code competitions; DPO more stable with lower compute overhead
- Industry trend 2025: DPO overtakes PPO for production alignment due to simplicity and reduced failure modes
- **Hugging Face TRL**: DPO is the default alignment method in TRL library; PPO support maintained but requires more tuning

## RLAIF: Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback

### Core Thesis (Bai et al., ICML 2024)
- Train reward model on preferences generated by an off-the-shelf strong LLM instead of human annotators
- **Scaling result**: RLAIF matches or exceeds RLHF on summarization and dialogue tasks when AI judge is sufficiently capable
- **Cost advantage**: human preference labeling ~$5-20K per dataset; AI feedback near-marginal compute cost
- **Risk**: reward hacking when AI judge has different failure modes than humans

### RLAIF in Production
- **Anthropic Claude 3**: first major deployment of RLAIF at scale (Constitutional AI + RLAIF)
- **AWS SageMaker RLAIF pipeline**: end-to-end implementation using Hugging Face Transformers + TRL
- **Curriculum-RLAIF (arXiv 2505.20075)**: addresses limited generalizability of conventional RLAIF reward models; introduces curriculum-based sample difficulty progression to mitigate distribution shift and preference label noise

## Alignment Tax

### Capability Degradation from Alignment
- Empirical studies show alignment fine-tuning reduces raw capability benchmarks by 3-15% depending on domain
- **Math/reasoning tasks**: largest tax (~10-15%), because alignment penalizes chain-of-thought verbosity and edge-case reasoning
- **Helpfulness regression**: aligned models refuse legitimate but borderline requests more often
- **OpenAI o1/o3 series**: partial reversal via extended reasoning at inference time — reasoning compute substitutes for some alignment tax

### Mitigation Strategies
- **Constitutional AI (Anthropic)**: self-critique + self-reflection reduces need for external reward model
- **Rejection sampling**: generate multiple completions, select best via reward model; avoids gradient-based alignment tax
- **Reversible alignment**: experimental; attempt to recover capability after alignment fine-tuning

## Reward Model Contamination & Over-Optimization

### Contamination Risk
- Reward models trained on AI-generated preference data inherit the judge's blind spots
- **Over-optimization**: policy model exploits reward model vulnerabilities rather than genuinely improving
- **Goodhart's Law**: once reward becomes a proxy for quality, models optimize the proxy not the underlying goal

### Defense Strategies
- **Reward model ensembles**: multiple independent RMs reduce single-model exploitation
- **Adversarial reward training**: include adversarial examples in RM training data
- **Uncertainty-aware RMs**: flag low-confidence predictions to prevent exploitation of RM blind spots

## Post-RLHF Frontier (2025-2026)

### Scaling RL Compute
- **Charlotte Xia Substack 2025 roundup**: post-training RL compute scaling is the dominant frontier
- OpenAI o1 (Sept 2024) -> DeepSeek R1 (Jan 2025) -> o3/GPT-5 series: reasoning models via massive RL compute budgets
- **Shift**: from alignment-focused RL to capability-focused RL (reasoning, tool use, long-horizon planning)

### Multi-Objective Alignment
- Tradeoff management between: helpfulness, honesty, harmlessness, capability
- **Pareto-optimal alignment**: no single optimal point; deployment context determines operating point
