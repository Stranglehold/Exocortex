## Overview

Rare earth elements (REEs) are 17 chemically similar metals critical to modern technology:
- **Light REEs:** Lanthanum, Cerium, Praseodymium, Neodymium, Promethium, Samarium
- **Heavy REEs:** Europium, Gadolinium, Terbium, Dysprosium, Holmium, Erbium, Thulium, Ytterbium, Lutetium
- **Scandium + Yttrium** (grouped with REEs despite not being lanthanides)

## Applications

| Application | REE Elements Used | Demand Driver |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent magnets (NdFeB) | Neodymium, Praseodymium, Dysprosium, Terbium | EV motors, wind turbines, robotics, defense systems |

| Catalysts | Lanthanum, Cerium | Petroleum refining |

## Export Control Architecture (2025-2026)

- **April 2025 (Wave 1):** Export restrictions on 7 rare earth elements and magnet materials targeting dysprosium, terbium, yttrium
- **October 2025 (Wave 2):** Expanded controls requiring export licenses for ANY parts/assemblies containing Chinese-sourced REEs OR produced with Chinese-sourced equipment — the "trace content" rule extends jurisdiction globally
- **November 2025 Suspension:** Beijing suspended 6 major export control directives through November 2026
- **Current status (May 2026):** Heavy REE exports from China down ~50% despite suspension. Licensing regime creates persistent friction even when suspended (Skillings analysis, FDD Mar 2026)
- **Assessment:** Suspension is a temporary reprieve, not a permanent solution. Western buyers treating it as one is a strategic error.

## The Processing Bottleneck — Key Insight

**The processing bottleneck is the real choke point, not mining.** China's dominance is in separation chemistry — the ability to take mixed rare earth ore and isolate individual elements at scale and low cost. Western mining projects that sell concentrate to Chinese refiners shift dependency upstream, not eliminate it.

### Heavy Rare Earth Deficit
- **Dysprosium and terbium** are critical for high-performance NdFeB magnets (EV motors, wind turbines, defense systems)
- China controls ~63% of global dysprosium reserves and the overwhelming majority of heavy REE separation capacity
- S&P Global Jan 2026: "Rare earth supply bottlenecks set to persist in 2026" — especially for heavy REEs used in high-performance technology

## Western Response & De-risking

### MP Materials (Mountain Pass, California)
- Largest REE deposit outside China. Produces concentrate.
- 2025: Accelerated vertical integration strategy — building domestic separation and magnet manufacturing
- Columbia Energy Policy Institute (Jul 2025): "MP Materials Deal Marks Significant Shift in US Rare Earths Policy"

### Lynas Rare Earths (Australia/Malaysia)
- Mt. Weld mine (WA) + separation facility in Malaysia (only major non-Chinese heavy REE separation hub)
- Building integrated rare earth plant in Texas (announced 2023, under construction)
- Middle East expansion: Saudi Arabia facility planned
- **Lynas is the single most important non-Chinese asset in the REE supply chain** — geographic concentration risk underappreciated

### EU Critical Raw Materials Act (CRMA)
- 10% of strategic raw materials mined within EU by 2030
- 40% processing capacity within EU by 2030
- 15% recycling rate for critical raw materials by 2030
- EU ECA Apr 2026: "Critical raw materials for the energy transition — not a rock solid policy" — notes significant implementation gaps

### FDD Assessment (March 2026)
- "Forging a New Critical Minerals Reality" — emphasizes need for domestic processing, not just mining
- Notes China's controls create persistent friction even when suspended
- Rare Earth Exchanges analysis: >50 institutions confirm modern industrial economies (especially Europe) are structurally dependent on supply chains governed by Beijing

## Recycling Economics

- Heavy REE recycling is a strategic option — bypasses primary mining entirely
- Current economic hurdles: dispersal, collection costs, insufficient scale
- Strategic premium on dysprosium and terbium rising fast enough that recycling may become viable sooner than market analysts expect
- Current recycling rates well below EU CRMA 15% target

## Defense Sector Implications

- NdFeB magnets critical for: F-35 actuators, Aegis combat system, guidance systems, sonar, jet engines
- US DOE and DoD critical materials lists include all 17 REEs as strategically vital
- China's export controls specifically designed to pressure defense-industrial base
- Stockpiling efforts: US DOE SPR, Japanese METI reserves — both insufficient for prolonged disruption
- **Critical vulnerability window: 2025-2028.** China's export controls are effective because:
  1. Processing concentration is higher than mining concentration
  2. Heavy REE alternatives don't exist at Western scale
  3. Magnet manufacturing (the final step) remains overwhelmingly Chinese despite upstream diversification

**Western progress is real but incomplete:** MP Materials and Lynas prove ex-China supply chains can work for light REEs. The bottleneck has moved downstream to heavy REE separation and magnet manufacturing.
